## Lecture Outline (Complex Exponentials and Phasor Interpretation)

### 1. Euler’s Formula
- Euler’s identity:
  $$ R e^{j\theta} = R\cos(\theta) + jR\sin(\theta) $$
- This separates a complex exponential into:
  - a **real part**
  - an **imaginary part**

---

### 2. Complex Exponential Signal
- General form:
  $$ z(t) = A e^{j(\omega_0 t + \phi)} $$
- Parameters:
  - $A$: magnitude
  - $\omega_0$: angular frequency in radians/second
  - $\phi$: phase angle

---

### 3. Rectangular Form Using Euler’s Formula
- Expanding the complex exponential:
  $$ z(t) = A\cos(\omega_0 t + \phi) + jA\sin(\omega_0 t + \phi) $$
- Therefore:
  $$ \Re\{z(t)\} = A\cos(\omega_0 t + \phi) $$
  $$ \Im\{z(t)\} = A\sin(\omega_0 t + \phi) $$

---

### 4. Interpretation in the Complex Plane
- The signal is represented in the complex plane with:
  - real axis
  - imaginary axis
- Magnitude and angle:
  $$ |z(t)| = A $$
  $$ \angle z(t) = \omega_0 t + \phi $$

---

### 5. Phasor at $t=0$
- At time $t=0$:
  $$ z(0) = A e^{j\phi} $$
- This is the **phasor** at the initial time.
- Interpretation:
  - magnitude is $A$
  - initial angle is $\phi$

---

### 6. Rotating Phasor
- As time increases, the phasor rotates **counterclockwise** in the complex plane.
- Angular speed of rotation:
  $$ \omega_0 \text{ rad/s} $$
- The magnitude stays constant, while the angle changes with time.

---

### 7. Real-Axis Projection
- The projection of the rotating phasor onto the real axis is:
  $$ \Re\{z(t)\} = A\cos(\omega_0 t + \phi) $$
- At $t=0$:
  $$ \Re\{z(0)\} = A\cos(\phi) $$
- When the total angle reaches:
  $$ \omega_0 t + \phi = \frac{\pi}{2} $$
  the real part becomes zero.

---

### 8. Imaginary-Axis Projection
- The projection onto the imaginary axis is:
  $$ \Im\{z(t)\} = A\sin(\omega_0 t + \phi) $$
- At $t=0$:
  $$ \Im\{z(0)\} = A\sin(\phi) $$
- When:
  $$ \omega_0 t + \phi = \frac{\pi}{2} $$
  the imaginary part is:
  $$ +A $$
- When:
  $$ \omega_0 t + \phi = \pi $$
  the imaginary part becomes zero.

---

### 9. Time-Domain View of the Rotating Phasor
- As the phasor rotates:
  - its real projection traces a cosine wave
  - its imaginary projection traces a sine wave
- This gives a direct connection between:
  - **phasor-domain representation**
  - **time-domain sinusoidal signals**

---

### 10. Key Insight
- A complex exponential is a rotating vector of constant magnitude.
- Its real and imaginary parts are sinusoids.
- This representation makes later signal-processing computations much easier.

---

### 11. Main Takeaways
- Complex exponential:
  $$ z(t) = A e^{j(\omega_0 t + \phi)} $$
- Real part:
  $$ A\cos(\omega_0 t + \phi) $$
- Imaginary part:
  $$ A\sin(\omega_0 t + \phi) $$
- The phasor rotates counterclockwise at:
  $$ \omega_0 \text{ rad/s} $$
- Complex exponentials provide a compact and powerful way to represent sinusoidal signals.
