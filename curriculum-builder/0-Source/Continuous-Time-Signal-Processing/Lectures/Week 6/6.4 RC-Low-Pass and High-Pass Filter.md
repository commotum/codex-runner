## Lecture Outline (Frequency Response of RC Low-Pass and High-Pass Filters)

### 1. Review: Frequency Response of an LTI System
- For an LTI system:
  $$ y(t) = x(t) * h(t) $$
- In convolution form:
  $$ y(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(\tau)\,x(t-\tau)\,d\tau $$
- If the input is a complex exponential:
  $$ x(t) = e^{j\omega t} $$
- Then the output is:
  $$ y(t) = H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t} $$
- Here:
  $$ H(j\omega) $$
  is the **frequency response** of the LTI system.

---

### 2. RC Circuit as an LTI System
- Consider a series $RC$ circuit with:
  - input:
    $$ x(t) = v_s(t) $$
  - output:
    $$ y(t) = v_C(t) $$
- The resistor and capacitor values are:
  $$ R, \quad C $$

---

### 3. RC Low-Pass Filter Setup
- Output is taken across the capacitor:
  $$ y(t) = v_C(t) $$
- Input is the source voltage:
  $$ x(t) = v_s(t) $$

---

### 4. Circuit Equation for the Low-Pass Filter
- Apply KVL:
  $$ v_s(t) = v_R(t) + v_C(t) $$
- Since:
  $$ v_R(t) = i(t)R $$
- And capacitor current is:
  $$ i(t) = C\frac{dv_C(t)}{dt} $$
- Substitute into KVL:
  $$ v_s(t) = RC\frac{dv_C(t)}{dt} + v_C(t) $$

---

### 5. Sinusoidal Input Assumption
- To find the frequency response, apply a sinusoidal / complex-exponential input:
  $$ v_s(t) = e^{j\omega t} $$
- Since the system is LTI, the output must be:
  $$ v_C(t) = H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t} $$

---

### 6. Deriving the Low-Pass Frequency Response
- Substitute the sinusoidal input and output into:
  $$ v_s(t) = RC\frac{dv_C(t)}{dt} + v_C(t) $$
- This gives:
  $$ e^{j\omega t} = RC\frac{d}{dt}\left(H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t}\right) + H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t} $$
- Differentiate:
  $$ e^{j\omega t} = j\omega RC\,H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t} + H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t} $$
- Cancel $e^{j\omega t}$:
  $$ 1 = H(j\omega)\left(1 + j\omega RC\right) $$
- Therefore:
  $$ H(j\omega) = \frac{1}{1 + j\omega RC} $$

---

### 7. Magnitude of the Low-Pass Filter
- Magnitude:
  $$ |H(j\omega)| = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + (\omega RC)^2}} $$
- At $\omega = 0$:
  $$ |H(j0)| = 1 $$
- As $\omega$ increases:
  $$ |H(j\omega)| \to 0 $$

---

### 8. Interpretation of the Low-Pass Response
- Low-frequency signals pass with little attenuation.
- High-frequency signals are attenuated.
- Therefore, this circuit acts as a **low-pass filter**.

---

### 9. Controlling the Low-Pass Cutoff
- The filter shape depends on the product:
  $$ RC $$
- By choosing $R$ and $C$, you can control:
  - how quickly the response rolls off
  - the cutoff frequency
- Larger or smaller $RC$ shifts the response in frequency.

---

### 10. RC High-Pass Filter Setup
- Use the same series $RC$ network, but now take the output across the resistor:
  $$ y(t) = v_R(t) $$
- Input remains:
  $$ x(t) = v_s(t) $$

---

### 11. Circuit Equation for the High-Pass Filter
- Start with KVL:
  $$ v_s(t) = v_R(t) + v_C(t) $$
- Also:
  $$ i(t) = \frac{v_R(t)}{R} $$
- Since:
  $$ v_C(t) = \frac{1}{C}\int i(t)\,dt $$
- Substitute:
  $$ v_s(t) = v_R(t) + \frac{1}{C}\int \frac{v_R(t)}{R}\,dt $$
- Differentiate both sides:
  $$ \frac{d}{dt}v_s(t) = \frac{d}{dt}v_R(t) + \frac{1}{RC}v_R(t) $$
- Multiply by $RC$:
  $$ RC\frac{d}{dt}v_s(t) = RC\frac{d}{dt}v_R(t) + v_R(t) $$

---

### 12. Sinusoidal Input for the High-Pass Filter
- Use:
  $$ v_s(t) = e^{j\omega t} $$
- Since the system is LTI:
  $$ v_R(t) = H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t} $$

---

### 13. Deriving the High-Pass Frequency Response
- Substitute into:
  $$ RC\frac{d}{dt}v_s(t) = RC\frac{d}{dt}v_R(t) + v_R(t) $$
- Then:
  $$ RC(j\omega)e^{j\omega t} = RC(j\omega)H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t} + H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t} $$
- Cancel $e^{j\omega t}$:
  $$ j\omega RC = H(j\omega)(1 + j\omega RC) $$
- Therefore:
  $$ H(j\omega) = \frac{j\omega RC}{1 + j\omega RC} $$

---

### 14. Magnitude of the High-Pass Filter
- Magnitude:
  $$ |H(j\omega)| = \frac{\omega RC}{\sqrt{1 + (\omega RC)^2}} $$
- At $\omega = 0$:
  $$ |H(j0)| = 0 $$
- As $\omega \to \infty$:
  $$ |H(j\omega)| \to 1 $$

---

### 15. Interpretation of the High-Pass Response
- Low-frequency signals are strongly attenuated.
- High-frequency signals pass through with little attenuation.
- Therefore, this circuit acts as a **high-pass filter**.

---

### 16. Comparing Low-Pass and High-Pass Filters
- **Low-pass filter**:
  $$ H(j\omega) = \frac{1}{1 + j\omega RC} $$
  - passes low frequencies
  - attenuates high frequencies

- **High-pass filter**:
  $$ H(j\omega) = \frac{j\omega RC}{1 + j\omega RC} $$
  - attenuates low frequencies
  - passes high frequencies

---

### 17. Main Takeaways
- A series $RC$ network can implement either:
  - a low-pass filter, or
  - a high-pass filter
- The only change is where the output is measured:
  - across the capacitor for low-pass
  - across the resistor for high-pass
- The product $RC$ determines the filter behavior and cutoff characteristics.
- Frequency response shows how the system changes the amplitude of sinusoidal inputs as a function of frequency.
