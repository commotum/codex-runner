## Lecture Outline (Exam Review: Linear Systems)

### 1. Exam Topics Overview

- Phasors
- Linear systems
- Impulse signals
- Transformations of signals in the time domain

---

### 2. Linear Systems

- System definition:
  $$ y[n] = \mathcal{H}\{x[n]\} $$
- A system is **linear** if it satisfies:
  - Homogeneity (scaling)
  - Additivity

---

### 3. Homogeneity (Scaling Property)

- Definition:
  $$ \mathcal{H}\{c\,x[n]\} = c\,\mathcal{H}\{x[n]\} $$
- Counterexample:
  $$ y[n] = \sin(x[n]) $$
  - Since $\sin(cx[n]) \neq c\sin(x[n])$, the system is **not homogeneous**
- Example:
  $$ y[n] = x[n]\sin(\omega_0 n) $$
  - Satisfies homogeneity

---

### 4. Additivity

- Definition:
  $$ \mathcal{H}\{x_1[n] + x_2[n]\} = \mathcal{H}\{x_1[n]\} + \mathcal{H}\{x_2[n]\} $$
- Counterexample:
  $$ y[n] = e^{j\omega_0 n}(1 + x[n]) $$
  - Not additive
- Trivial additive system:
  $$ y[n] = 0 $$

---

### 5. Convolution Systems

- System defined by convolution:
  $$ y[n] = \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} x[m]\,h[n-m] $$
- Properties:
  - Additive
  - Homogeneous
- Conclusion: convolution systems are **linear**

---

### 6. Superposition Principle

- A system is linear **if and only if**:
  $$ \mathcal{H}\{a x_1[n] + b x_2[n]\}
     = a\mathcal{H}\{x_1[n]\} + b\mathcal{H}\{x_2[n]\} $$

---

### 7. Nonlinear System Example

- System:
  $$ y[n] = x[n]\,x[n-1] $$
- Applying superposition produces cross-terms
- Conclusion: system is **not linear**

---

### 8. Time-Invariant Systems

- Definition:
  - If input $x[n] \to y[n]$, then
    $$ x[n-m] \to y[n-m] $$
- Formal condition:
  $$ \mathcal{H}\{x[n-m]\} = y[n-m] $$

---

### 9. Time-Variance Example

- System:
  $$ y[n] = \sin(\omega_0 n)\,x[n] $$
- Time-shifted input:
  $$ \mathcal{H}\{x[n-m]\} = \sin(\omega_0 n)\,x[n-m] $$
- Expected output:
  $$ y[n-m] = \sin(\omega_0(n-m))\,x[n-m] $$
- Not equal → system is **time-varying**

---

### 10. Time Invariance of Convolution Systems

- Given:
  $$ y[n] = \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} x[m]\,h[n-m] $$
- Time-shifted input:
  $$ w[n] = x[n-k] $$
- Result:
  $$ y_w[n] = y[n-k] $$
- Conclusion: convolution systems are **time invariant**
