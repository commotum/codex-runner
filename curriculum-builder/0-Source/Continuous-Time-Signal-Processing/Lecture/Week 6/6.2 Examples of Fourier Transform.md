## Lecture Outline (Fourier Transform Examples: Exponential, Pulse, and Impulse)

### 1. Review of the Fourier Transform
- Fourier transform:
  $$ X(j\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- Inverse Fourier transform:
  $$ x(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} X(j\omega)e^{j\omega t}\,d\omega $$
- Main idea:
  - move from the **time domain** to the **frequency domain**
  - represent a general aperiodic signal using a continuous spectrum of frequencies

---

### 2. Generalized Exponential Example
- Time-domain signal:
  $$ x(t) = e^{-at}u(t) $$
- Fourier transform pair:
  $$ e^{-at}u(t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \frac{1}{a + j\omega} $$

---

### 3. Real and Imaginary Parts of the Exponential Transform
- Rewrite:
  $$ X(j\omega) = \frac{1}{a + j\omega} $$
- Multiply numerator and denominator by the complex conjugate:
  $$ X(j\omega) = \frac{a - j\omega}{a^2 + \omega^2} $$
- Separate into real and imaginary parts:
  $$ X(j\omega) = \frac{a}{a^2 + \omega^2} - j\frac{\omega}{a^2 + \omega^2} $$
- Therefore:
  - real part:
    $$ \Re\{X(j\omega)\} = \frac{a}{a^2 + \omega^2} $$
  - imaginary part:
    $$ \Im\{X(j\omega)\} = -\frac{\omega}{a^2 + \omega^2} $$

---

### 4. Frequency-Domain Interpretation of the Exponential
- The original signal:
  $$ x(t)=e^{-at}u(t) $$
  is one-sided and decays in time.
- Its Fourier transform is continuous in frequency:
  $$ X(j\omega)=\frac{1}{a+j\omega} $$
- The real part decays as $|\omega|$ increases.

---

### 5. Rectangular Pulse Example
- Time-domain signal:
  $$
  x(t)=
  \begin{cases}
  1, & |t| \le \frac{T}{2} \\
  0, & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
  $$
- Fourier transform:
  $$ X(j\omega)=\int_{-T/2}^{T/2} e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$

---

### 6. Fourier Transform of the Rectangular Pulse
- Evaluating the integral gives:
  $$ X(j\omega)=\frac{e^{-j\omega T/2}-e^{j\omega T/2}}{-j\omega} $$
- Using Euler identities:
  $$ X(j\omega)=\frac{\sin(\omega T/2)}{\omega/2} $$
- This is the **sinc-shaped spectrum**.

---

### 7. Value at $\omega = 0$
- At $\omega=0$, the expression appears undefined.
- Use L’Hospital’s rule:
  $$ \lim_{\omega \to 0}\frac{\sin(\omega T/2)}{\omega/2} = T $$
- Therefore:
  $$ X(j0)=T $$

---

### 8. Shape of the Pulse Spectrum
- The Fourier transform of the rectangular pulse is oscillatory in frequency.
- It has:
  - peak value:
    $$ T $$
  - zeros at:
    $$ \omega = \pm \frac{2\pi}{T}, \pm \frac{4\pi}{T}, \dots $$
- This is called the **sinc function**.

---

### 9. Pulse Written Using Unit Step Functions
- The same pulse can be written as:
  $$ x(t)=u\left(t+\frac{T}{2}\right)-u\left(t-\frac{T}{2}\right) $$
- So the Fourier transform pair is:
  $$ u\left(t+\frac{T}{2}\right)-u\left(t-\frac{T}{2}\right)
     \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
     \frac{\sin(\omega T/2)}{\omega/2} $$

---

### 10. Impulse in the Time Domain
- Time-domain signal:
  $$ x(t)=\delta(t) $$
- Fourier transform:
  $$ X(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- Since the impulse samples the integrand at $t=0$:
  $$ X(j\omega)=1 $$
- Therefore:
  $$ \delta(t)\;\Longleftrightarrow\; 1 $$

---

### 11. Interpretation of the Impulse Transform
- In time, the impulse is concentrated entirely at:
  $$ t=0 $$
- In frequency, it is spread uniformly across all frequencies:
  $$ X(j\omega)=1 $$
- Interpretation:
  - all frequencies contribute equally to form the impulse

---

### 12. Impulse in the Frequency Domain
- Now define an impulse in frequency:
  $$ X(j\omega)=2\pi\delta(\omega) $$
- Apply the inverse Fourier transform:
  $$ x(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} 2\pi\delta(\omega)e^{j\omega t}\,d\omega $$
- Using the sifting property:
  $$ x(t)=1 $$
- Therefore:
  $$ 1 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; 2\pi\delta(\omega) $$

---

### 13. Interpretation of the DC Signal Transform
- A constant signal in time:
  $$ x(t)=1 $$
  has only one frequency component: **DC**
- So in the frequency domain, all of its energy is concentrated at:
  $$ \omega=0 $$
- That is why its transform is an impulse:
  $$ 2\pi\delta(\omega) $$

---

### 14. Main Fourier Transform Pairs from This Lecture
- Exponential:
  $$ e^{-at}u(t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \frac{1}{a+j\omega} $$
- Rectangular pulse:
  $$ u\left(t+\frac{T}{2}\right)-u\left(t-\frac{T}{2}\right)
     \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
     \frac{\sin(\omega T/2)}{\omega/2} $$
- Time-domain impulse:
  $$ \delta(t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; 1 $$
- Constant signal:
  $$ 1 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; 2\pi\delta(\omega) $$

---

### 15. Key Takeaways
- Fourier transform converts a time-domain signal into its continuous frequency spectrum.
- A one-sided exponential gives a rational frequency response:
  $$ \frac{1}{a+j\omega} $$
- A rectangular pulse gives a sinc-shaped spectrum:
  $$ \frac{\sin(\omega T/2)}{\omega/2} $$
- A time impulse spreads equally across all frequencies:
  $$ \delta(t)\leftrightarrow 1 $$
- A constant time-domain signal becomes a frequency-domain impulse at DC:
  $$ 1\leftrightarrow 2\pi\delta(\omega) $$
