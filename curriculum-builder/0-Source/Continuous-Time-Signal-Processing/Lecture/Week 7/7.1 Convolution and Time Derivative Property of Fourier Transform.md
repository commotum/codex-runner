## Lecture Outline (RC Low-Pass and High-Pass Filters via Frequency Response)

### 1. Review: Frequency Response of an LTI System
- For an LTI system with impulse response $h(t)$, the output is:
  $$ y(t) = x(t) * h(t) $$
- In convolution form:
  $$ y(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(\tau)\,x(t-\tau)\,d\tau $$
- If the input is a complex exponential:
  $$ x(t) = e^{j\omega t} $$
- Then the output is:
  $$ y(t) = H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t} $$
- Interpretation:
  - the output has the **same frequency** $\omega$
  - the amplitude and phase are modified by the factor $H(j\omega)$

---

### 2. Frequency Response Formula
- The frequency response of an LTI system is:
  $$ H(j\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- This is also the Fourier transform of the impulse response $h(t)$.

---

### 3. RC Low-Pass Filter Setup
- Circuit:
  - source voltage input $v_s(t)$
  - resistor $R$
  - capacitor $C$
  - output taken across the capacitor:
    $$ y(t) = v_C(t) $$
- Input:
  $$ x(t) = v_s(t) $$

---

### 4. Low-Pass Filter Circuit Equation
- Apply KVL:
  $$ v_s(t) = v_R(t) + v_C(t) $$
- Since:
  $$ v_R(t) = i(t)R $$
- And for the capacitor:
  $$ i(t) = C\frac{dv_C(t)}{dt} $$
- Substitute into KVL:
  $$ v_s(t) = RC\frac{dv_C(t)}{dt} + v_C(t) $$

---

### 5. Sinusoidal Input Assumption for the Low-Pass Filter
- Use a complex-exponential input:
  $$ v_s(t) = e^{j\omega t} $$
- Since the system is LTI, assume the output is:
  $$ v_C(t) = H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t} $$

---

### 6. Derivation of the Low-Pass Frequency Response
- Substitute into the circuit equation:
  $$ e^{j\omega t} = RC\frac{d}{dt}\left(H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t}\right) + H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t} $$
- Differentiate:
  $$ e^{j\omega t} = j\omega RC\,H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t} + H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t} $$
- Cancel $e^{j\omega t}$:
  $$ 1 = H(j\omega)\left(1 + j\omega RC\right) $$
- Therefore:
  $$ H(j\omega) = \frac{1}{1 + j\omega RC} $$

---

### 7. Magnitude Response of the Low-Pass Filter
- The magnitude is:
  $$ |H(j\omega)| = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}} $$
- Behavior:
  - at $\omega = 0$:
    $$ |H(j0)| = 1 $$
  - as $\omega$ increases:
    $$ |H(j\omega)| \to 0 $$

---

### 8. Interpretation of the Low-Pass Filter
- Low-frequency inputs pass with little attenuation.
- High-frequency inputs are attenuated.
- Therefore, the RC circuit with output across the capacitor acts as a **low-pass filter**.

---

### 9. Controlling the Cutoff Behavior
- The filter behavior depends on the product:
  $$ RC $$
- By choosing $R$ and $C$, the cutoff and roll-off behavior can be adjusted.
- Larger or smaller values of $RC$ change how sharply the filter transitions.

---

### 10. RC High-Pass Filter Setup
- Same series $RC$ circuit, but now the output is taken across the resistor:
  $$ y(t) = v_R(t) $$
- Input remains:
  $$ x(t) = v_s(t) $$

---

### 11. High-Pass Filter Circuit Equation
- Start with:
  $$ v_s(t) = v_R(t) + v_C(t) $$
- Use:
  $$ i(t) = \frac{v_R(t)}{R} $$
- And capacitor voltage:
  $$ v_C(t) = \frac{1}{C}\int i(t)\,dt $$
- Substitute:
  $$ v_s(t) = v_R(t) + \frac{1}{C}\int \frac{v_R(t)}{R}\,dt $$
- Differentiate both sides:
  $$ \frac{d}{dt}v_s(t) = \frac{d}{dt}v_R(t) + \frac{1}{RC}v_R(t) $$
- Multiply by $RC$:
  $$ RC\frac{d}{dt}v_s(t) = RC\frac{d}{dt}v_R(t) + v_R(t) $$

---

### 12. Sinusoidal Input Assumption for the High-Pass Filter
- Use:
  $$ v_s(t) = e^{j\omega t} $$
- Since the system is LTI, let:
  $$ v_R(t) = H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t} $$

---

### 13. Derivation of the High-Pass Frequency Response
- Substitute into the differential equation:
  $$ RC\frac{d}{dt}v_s(t) = RC\frac{d}{dt}v_R(t) + v_R(t) $$
- This gives:
  $$ RC(j\omega)e^{j\omega t} = RC(j\omega)H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t} + H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t} $$
- Cancel $e^{j\omega t}$:
  $$ j\omega RC = H(j\omega)(1 + j\omega RC) $$
- Therefore:
  $$ H(j\omega) = \frac{j\omega RC}{1 + j\omega RC} $$

---

### 14. Magnitude Response of the High-Pass Filter
- The magnitude is:
  $$ |H(j\omega)| = \frac{\omega RC}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}} $$
- Behavior:
  - at $\omega = 0$:
    $$ |H(j0)| = 0 $$
  - for large $\omega$:
    $$ |H(j\omega)| \to 1 $$

---

### 15. Interpretation of the High-Pass Filter
- Low-frequency inputs are strongly attenuated.
- High-frequency inputs pass with little attenuation.
- Therefore, the RC circuit with output across the resistor acts as a **high-pass filter**.

---

### 16. Comparing the Two RC Filters
- **Low-pass output across capacitor**:
  $$ H(j\omega) = \frac{1}{1+j\omega RC} $$
- **High-pass output across resistor**:
  $$ H(j\omega) = \frac{j\omega RC}{1+j\omega RC} $$

- Low-pass filter:
  - passes low frequencies
  - rejects high frequencies

- High-pass filter:
  - rejects low frequencies
  - passes high frequencies

---

### 17. Main Takeaways
- A sinusoidal input to an LTI system produces a sinusoidal output with the same frequency.
- The output amplitude and phase are determined by the frequency response $H(j\omega)$.
- A series RC network can be used to realize:
  - a **low-pass filter** if output is taken across the capacitor
  - a **high-pass filter** if output is taken across the resistor
- The design of the filter depends on the values of $R$ and $C$.
