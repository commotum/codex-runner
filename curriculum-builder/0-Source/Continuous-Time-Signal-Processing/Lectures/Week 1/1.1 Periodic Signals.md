## Lecture Outline (Periodic Signals, Harmonics, and Periodicity of Signal Combinations)

### 1. Periodic Continuous-Time Signals
- A continuous-time signal is periodic if it repeats after some positive time $T$:
  $$ x(t) = x(t+T), \quad T>0 $$
- More generally, the repetition condition can be written as:
  $$ x(t) = x(t+nT), \quad n \in \mathbb{Z} $$
- $T$ is called the **fundamental period**.

---

### 2. Example: Periodic Pulse Train
- A pulse train that repeats every $2$ seconds has period:
  $$ T = 2 \text{ s} $$
- The signal also repeats after any integer multiple of the period:
  $$ x(t) = x(t+nT) $$

---

### 3. Real-Valued Sinusoidal Signals
- General form:
  $$ x(t) = A\sin(\omega_0 t+\phi) $$
- Parameters:
  - $A$: amplitude
  - $\omega_0$: fundamental angular frequency in radians/second
  - $\phi$: phase angle in radians
- Relationship between period and angular frequency:
  $$ \omega_0 = \frac{2\pi}{T} $$

---

### 4. Physical Examples of Sinusoidal Behavior
- Sinusoids appear naturally in many real systems, including:
  - Generator / AC voltage waveforms
  - Undamped mass-spring oscillations
- These examples motivate why sinusoidal signals are so important in signals and systems.

---

### 5. Role of Phase
- The phase $\phi$ determines where the sinusoid starts at $t=0$.
- Changing the phase shifts the waveform in time.
- Phase can also convert between sine and cosine forms.

---

### 6. Harmonics
- Harmonics are sinusoidal components whose frequencies are integer multiples of a fundamental frequency:
  $$ \omega_k = k\omega_0, \quad k=1,2,3,\dots $$
- The $k$th harmonic oscillates faster than the fundamental by a factor of $k$.

---

### 7. Harmonic Examples
- First harmonic:
  $$ x_1(t) = \frac{1}{2}\cos(2\pi t), \quad \omega_1 = 1\cdot 2\pi $$
- Second harmonic:
  $$ x_2(t) = \cos(4\pi t), \quad \omega_2 = 2\cdot 2\pi = 4\pi $$
- Third harmonic:
  $$ x_3(t) = \frac{1}{3}\cos(6\pi t), \quad \omega_3 = 3\cdot 2\pi = 6\pi $$

---

### 8. Why Harmonics Matter
- Periodic signals can be represented as sums of harmonically related sinusoids.
- This leads directly into later topics such as Fourier series and Fourier transforms.

---

### 9. Periodicity of the Sum of Two Periodic Signals
- Suppose:
  $$ x(t) = x(t+mT_1) $$
  $$ y(t) = y(t+nT_2) $$
  where $m,n \in \mathbb{Z}$.
- Form a linear combination:
  $$ z(t) = ax(t) + by(t) $$
- For $z(t)$ to be periodic with period $T$, the periods must satisfy:
  $$ T = mT_1 = nT_2 $$
- Equivalently:
  $$ \frac{T_1}{T_2} = \frac{n}{m} $$
- Therefore, the sum of two periodic signals is periodic if the ratio of their periods is **rational**.

---

### 10. Linear vs. Nonlinear Operations
- Addition is a **linear** operation:
  $$ z(t) = ax(t)+by(t) $$
- Multiplication is a **nonlinear** operation:
  $$ z(t) = x(t)y(t) $$

---

### 11. Product of Two Sinusoids
- Let:
  $$ x(t) = \cos(\omega_1 t), \quad y(t) = \cos(\omega_2 t) $$
- Then:
  $$ z(t) = \cos(\omega_1 t)\cos(\omega_2 t) $$
- Using the product-to-sum identity:
  $$ z(t) = \frac{1}{2}\cos((\omega_1-\omega_2)t) + \frac{1}{2}\cos((\omega_1+\omega_2)t) $$

---

### 12. Equal-Frequency Case
- If both signals have the same period $T$, then:
  $$ \omega_1 = \omega_2 = \omega = \frac{2\pi}{T} $$
- The product becomes:
  $$ z(t) = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\cos(2\omega t) $$
- This contains:
  - a DC term:
    $$ \frac{1}{2} $$
  - a second harmonic:
    $$ \frac{1}{2}\cos(2\omega t) $$

---

### 13. Key Takeaway About Nonlinear Operations
- Nonlinear operations can generate higher-order harmonics even when the original signals only contain the fundamental frequency.
- In this example, multiplying two sinusoids at frequency $\omega$ produces a component at $2\omega$.

---

### 14. Main Conclusions
- A continuous-time signal is periodic if:
  $$ x(t) = x(t+nT) $$
- A sinusoid satisfies:
  $$ \omega_0 = \frac{2\pi}{T} $$
- Harmonics are given by:
  $$ \omega_k = k\omega_0 $$
- The sum of periodic signals is periodic if their period ratio is rational.
- Nonlinear operations such as multiplication can create new harmonic components.
