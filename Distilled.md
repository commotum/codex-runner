## The Importance of Active Learning and Direct Instruction

Direct instruction and active learning solve complementary problems that every student faces in a traditional lecture environment. **Direct instruction** removes ambiguity by making important moves and skills explicit, showing the learner exactly what to do, when to do it, and what a correct solution looks like. It teaches knowledge to students explicitly as opposed to attempting to have students “construct their own knowledge” through unguided activities. 

**Active learning** forces the acquisition of competence, not just the illusion of it, by requiring the completion of individualized training activities specially chosen to improve specific aspects of one’s performance through repetition and successive refinement. This form of targeted deliberate practice is the opposite of mindless repetition, and has been shown to be one of the most prominent underlying factors responsible for individual differences in performance, even among highly talented elite performers.

In an ideal lesson, these two are composed into an **Active-Learning Loop**: the lesson states the skill clearly, models it with a worked example, then immediately asks the learner to do the same move through closely matched practice, answer checks, and later independent problems; this combination keeps cognitive load focused on the intended idea, exposes confusion early, strengthens memory through use, and accelerates productive learning by turning explanation into action before the learner’s understanding fades.

### Active Learning Loop

1. **Observe**  
   Observe a new task being done correctly.

2. **Attempt**  
   Attempt the task on your own.

3. **Feedback**  
   Get feedback on your attempt.

4. **Master**  
   Practice the task correctly until mastery.

5. **Repeat**  
   Return to Step 1 with a new task just beyond your current mastery.

Repeat this cycle/progression continuously to rapidly improve understanding and performance.
