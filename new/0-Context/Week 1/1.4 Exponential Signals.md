## Lecture Outline (Generalized Exponential Signals and Periodicity of Complex Exponentials)

### 1. Generalized Exponential Signal
- Define the signal:
  $$ x(t) = e^{st} $$
- Complex exponent:
  $$ s = \sigma + j\omega $$
- Here:
  - $\sigma$ is the real part
  - $\omega$ is the imaginary-frequency component

---

### 2. Expanding the Generalized Exponential
- Substitute $s=\sigma + j\omega$:
  $$ x(t) = e^{(\sigma + j\omega)t} = e^{\sigma t} e^{j\omega t} $$
- Using Euler’s formula:
  $$ e^{j\theta} = \cos(\theta) + j\sin(\theta) $$
- Therefore:
  $$ x(t) = e^{\sigma t}\big(\cos(\omega t) + j\sin(\omega t)\big) $$

---

### 3. Real and Imaginary Parts
- Expanding further:
  $$ x(t) = e^{\sigma t}\cos(\omega t) + j e^{\sigma t}\sin(\omega t) $$
- Real part:
  $$ \Re\{x(t)\} = e^{\sigma t}\cos(\omega t) $$
- Imaginary part:
  $$ \Im\{x(t)\} = e^{\sigma t}\sin(\omega t) $$

---

### 4. Case 1: $\omega = 0$
- If $\omega = 0$, then:
  $$ x(t) = e^{\sigma t} $$
- This is a purely real exponential signal.

#### Behavior for different values of $\sigma$
- If $\sigma = 0$:
  $$ x(t) = 1 $$
  - constant signal

- If $\sigma > 0$:
  $$ x(t) = e^{\sigma t} $$
  - exponentially growing signal

- If $\sigma < 0$:
  $$ x(t) = e^{\sigma t} $$
  - exponentially decaying signal

---

### 5. Case 2: $\sigma = 0$, $\omega \ne 0$
- If $\sigma = 0$, then:
  $$ x(t) = e^{j\omega t} $$
- Using Euler’s formula:
  $$ x(t) = \cos(\omega t) + j\sin(\omega t) $$
- This is a purely oscillatory complex sinusoid.

#### Components
- Real part:
  $$ \cos(\omega t) $$
- Imaginary part:
  $$ \sin(\omega t) $$

---

### 6. Behavior When Only $\omega$ Changes
- Changing $\omega$ changes the oscillation rate.
- Larger $|\omega|$ means faster oscillation.
- Smaller $|\omega|$ means slower oscillation.

---

### 7. Case 3: $\sigma \ne 0$ and $\omega \ne 0$
- General form:
  $$ x(t) = e^{\sigma t}\cos(\omega t) + j e^{\sigma t}\sin(\omega t) $$
- This combines:
  - exponential growth/decay from $e^{\sigma t}$
  - oscillation from $\cos(\omega t)$ and $\sin(\omega t)$

---

### 8. Damped and Growing Sinusoids
#### If $\sigma < 0$
- The signal oscillates, but its amplitude decays over time.
- Real part:
  $$ \Re\{x(t)\} = e^{\sigma t}\cos(\omega t), \quad \sigma < 0 $$
- This is a **damped sinusoid**.

#### If $\sigma > 0$
- The signal oscillates, but its amplitude grows over time.
- Real part:
  $$ \Re\{x(t)\} = e^{\sigma t}\cos(\omega t), \quad \sigma > 0 $$
- This is a **growing sinusoid**.

---

### 9. Envelope Interpretation
- The oscillation is bounded by exponential envelopes:
  $$ \pm e^{\sigma t} $$
- So the sinusoid sits inside the curves:
  $$ x_{\text{env}}(t) = \pm e^{\sigma t} $$

---

### 10. Physical Interpretation
- Pure oscillation corresponds to undamped sinusoidal behavior.
- Adding exponential decay models damping in real systems.
- Example mentioned in the lecture:
  - oscillatory circuit behavior with damping due to resistance

---

### 11. Complex Exponential Periodicity
- Consider:
  $$ x(t) = e^{j\omega t} $$
- For periodicity, require:
  $$ x(t+T) = x(t) $$
- Substitute:
  $$ e^{j\omega (t+T)} = e^{j\omega t} $$
- Rearranging:
  $$ e^{j\omega t} e^{j\omega T} = e^{j\omega t} $$
- So the condition becomes:
  $$ e^{j\omega T} = 1 $$

---

### 12. Fundamental Period of the Complex Exponential
- Since:
  $$ e^{j\theta} = 1 \quad \text{when} \quad \theta = 2\pi k,\; k\in\mathbb{Z} $$
- The periodicity condition is:
  $$ \omega T = 2\pi k $$
- For the fundamental period, take $k=1$:
  $$ T_0 = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} $$

---

### 13. General Repetition Condition
- More generally:
  $$ \omega T = 2\pi k, \quad k \in \mathbb{Z} $$
- So the complex exponential repeats after any integer multiple of the fundamental period.

---

### 14. Geometric Interpretation
- The signal:
  $$ e^{j\omega t} $$
  can be viewed as a rotating phasor on the unit circle.
- One full revolution corresponds to:
  $$ 2\pi \text{ radians} $$
- After one full revolution, the phasor returns to the same point, so the signal repeats.

---

### 15. Main Takeaways
- A generalized exponential signal is:
  $$ x(t) = e^{st}, \quad s=\sigma + j\omega $$
- It can be written as:
  $$ x(t) = e^{\sigma t}\cos(\omega t) + j e^{\sigma t}\sin(\omega t) $$
- $\sigma$ controls growth or decay.
- $\omega$ controls oscillation frequency.
- If $\sigma=0$, the signal is purely sinusoidal.
- If $\omega=0$, the signal is purely exponential.
- If both are nonzero, the result is an oscillating signal with an exponential envelope.
- A pure complex exponential is periodic with:
  $$ T_0 = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} $$
