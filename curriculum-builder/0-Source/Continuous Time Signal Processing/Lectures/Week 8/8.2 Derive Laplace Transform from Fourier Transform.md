## Lecture Outline (Deriving the Laplace Transform from the Fourier Transform)

### 1. Review of the Fourier Transform
- The Fourier transform of a time-domain signal $x(t)$ is:
  $$ X(j\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- This transforms a signal from the **time domain** to the **frequency domain**.

---

### 2. When the Fourier Transform Works Well
- The Fourier transform works well for signals that decay to zero as $t \to \infty$ and $t \to -\infty$.
- Example idea:
  - signals like Gaussian-shaped signals decay at both ends
  - the integral converges over the whole real line

---

### 3. Signals for Which the Fourier Transform Fails
- Some signals do **not** decay as $t \to \infty$, so the Fourier transform integral may diverge.
- Examples discussed:
  - an exponentially growing signal such as $e^{\sigma t}$ with $\sigma > 0$
  - the unit step function $u(t)$
- For these signals:
  $$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
  may not converge.

---

### 4. The Key Laplace-Transform Trick
- To force convergence, multiply the signal by a decaying exponential:
  $$ e^{-\sigma t} $$
- Also restrict attention to causal signals using:
  $$ u(t) $$
- So the modified signal becomes:
  $$ f(t) = x(t)e^{-\sigma t}u(t) $$

---

### 5. Apply the Fourier Transform to the Modified Signal
- Take the Fourier transform of $f(t)$:
  $$ F(j\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- Since $u(t)$ makes the signal zero for $t<0$, this becomes:
  $$ F(j\omega) = \int_0^{\infty} x(t)e^{-\sigma t}e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- Combine the exponentials:
  $$ F(j\omega) = \int_0^{\infty} x(t)e^{-(\sigma + j\omega)t}\,dt $$

---

### 6. Define the Laplace Variable
- Introduce the complex variable:
  $$ s = \sigma + j\omega $$
- Then the transform becomes:
  $$ X(s) = \int_0^{\infty} x(t)e^{-st}\,dt $$
- This is the **one-sided (unilateral) Laplace transform**.

---

### 7. Unilateral Laplace Transform
- Standard one-sided Laplace transform:
  $$ X(s) = \int_0^{\infty} x(t)e^{-st}\,dt $$
- Often written as:
  $$ X(s) = \int_{0^-}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-st}\,dt $$
- The notation $0^-$ is used to include singularities such as impulses at $t=0$.

---

### 8. Laplace Transform as a Generalization of Fourier Transform
- Since:
  $$ s = \sigma + j\omega $$
- If the real part is zero, i.e.
  $$ \sigma = 0 $$
  then:
  $$ s = j\omega $$
- So the Laplace transform reduces to the Fourier transform.
- This is why Laplace transform is called a **generalization** of the Fourier transform.

---

### 9. Intuition Behind Why Laplace Helps
- In the factor:
  $$ e^{-st} = e^{-(\sigma + j\omega)t} = e^{-\sigma t}e^{-j\omega t} $$
- The term:
  $$ e^{-\sigma t} $$
  can add decay to the integrand.
- If $\sigma > 0$, this helps the transform converge even when the original Fourier transform would not exist.

---

### 10. Example Signal
- Consider the causal exponential:
  $$ x(t) = e^{-at}u(t), \qquad a > 0 $$

---

### 11. Fourier Transform of the Example
- Since the signal is causal:
  $$ X(j\omega) = \int_0^{\infty} e^{-at}e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- Combine the exponentials:
  $$ X(j\omega) = \int_0^{\infty} e^{-(a+j\omega)t}\,dt $$
- Evaluating the integral:
  $$ X(j\omega) = \frac{1}{a + j\omega} $$
- This exists because $a>0$, so the signal decays.

---

### 12. Laplace Transform of the Example
- Apply the Laplace transform:
  $$ X(s) = \int_0^{\infty} e^{-at}e^{-st}\,dt $$
- Combine terms:
  $$ X(s) = \int_0^{\infty} e^{-(s+a)t}\,dt $$
- Evaluate:
  $$ X(s) = \frac{1}{s+a} $$

---

### 13. Region of Convergence (ROC)
- The expression
  $$ \frac{1}{s+a} $$
  is only valid when the integral converges.
- Convergence requires:
  $$ \Re\{s+a\} > 0 $$
- Therefore:
  $$ \Re\{s\} > -a $$
- This condition is called the **region of convergence** (ROC).

---

### 14. The $s$-Plane
- Since:
  $$ s = \sigma + j\omega $$
- The $s$-plane has:
  - horizontal axis: $\sigma$
  - vertical axis: $j\omega$
- For the example:
  $$ X(s) = \frac{1}{s+a} $$
  the ROC is the half-plane:
  $$ \sigma > -a $$

---

### 15. Main Takeaways
- Fourier transform:
  $$ X(j\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- One-sided Laplace transform:
  $$ X(s) = \int_0^{\infty} x(t)e^{-st}\,dt $$
- Laplace transform is obtained by introducing exponential weighting:
  $$ e^{-\sigma t} $$
- The Laplace transform generalizes the Fourier transform because:
  $$ s = \sigma + j\omega $$
  and Fourier is recovered when:
  $$ \sigma = 0 $$
- Example transform pair:
  $$ e^{-at}u(t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \frac{1}{s+a}, \qquad \Re\{s\} > -a $$
