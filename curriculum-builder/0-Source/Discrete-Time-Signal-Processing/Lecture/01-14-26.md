## Lecture Outline

### 1. Sinusoids and Complex Exponentials

- Representation of sinusoids using complex exponentials:
  $$ x(t) = 5e^{j(6t+\phi_1)} + 2e^{j(6t+\phi_2)} $$
- Factoring common frequency terms:
  $$ x(t) = e^{j6t}\left(5e^{j\phi_1} + 2e^{j\phi_2}\right) $$
- Phasor interpretation at $t=0$:
  $$ 5e^{j\phi_1}, \quad 2e^{j\phi_2} $$
- Vector (phasor) addition in the complex plane

---

### 2. Amplitude and Phase of Summed Sinusoids

- Real and imaginary components:
  $$ \Re\{A\} = 5\cos\phi_1 + 2\cos\phi_2 $$
  $$ \Im\{A\} = 5\sin\phi_1 + 2\sin\phi_2 $$
- Resulting amplitude:
  $$ A = \sqrt{(5\cos\phi_1 + 2\cos\phi_2)^2 + (5\sin\phi_1 + 2\sin\phi_2)^2} $$
- Resulting phase:
  $$ \theta = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{5\sin\phi_1 + 2\sin\phi_2}{5\cos\phi_1 + 2\cos\phi_2}\right) $$

---

### 3. Trigonometric and Exponential Signal Conversion

- Converting between sine, cosine, and exponential forms:
  $$ 5\cos(6t+\tfrac{\pi}{2}) + 2\sin(6t) $$
- Expressing sinusoids as real parts of exponentials:
  $$ \Re\{5e^{j(6t+\pi/2)} + 2e^{j(6t-\pi/2)}\} $$

---

### 4. Complex Number Multiplication and Polar Form

- Example multiplication:
  $$ (4+3j)(6+8j) = 50j $$
- Conversion to polar form:
  $$ 4+3j = 5e^{j\tan^{-1}(3/4)} $$
  $$ 6+8j = 10e^{j\tan^{-1}(4/3)} $$
- Result:
  $$ 50e^{j\pi/2} $$

---

### 5. Systems and Signal Operations

- System representation:
  $$ y[n] = \mathcal{H}\{x[n]\} $$
- Interpretation of systems as mappings from input signals to output signals

---

### 6. Time Shifting

- Definition:
  $$ y[n] = x[n-m] $$
- Effects:
  - $m>0$: delay
  - $m<0$: advance
- Examples with exponentials and sinusoids

---

### 7. Time Reversal

- Definition:
  $$ y[n] = x[-n] $$
- Effect on signal symmetry and indexing

---

### 8. Sampling Rate Changes

- Subsampling:
  $$ y[n] = x[Mn], \quad M \in \mathbb{Z} $$
- Upsampling:
  $$
  y[n] =
  \begin{cases}
    x[n/M], & n \text{ multiple of } M \\
    0, & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
  $$

---

### 9. Unit Impulse Signal

- Definition:
  $$
  \delta[n] =
  \begin{cases}
    1, & n=0 \\
    0, & n\neq 0
  \end{cases}
  $$
- Shifted impulse:
  $$ \delta[n-k] $$

---

### 10. Properties of the Unit Impulse

- Summation property:
  $$ \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} \delta[m] = 1 $$
- Sifting property:
  $$ \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} x[m]\delta[m-k] = x[k] $$

---

### 11. Impulse Decomposition of Signals

- Decomposition of any discrete-time signal:
  $$ x[n] = \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} x[m]\delta[n-m] $$
- Interpretation as weighted and shifted impulses

---

### 12. System Properties

- Linearity:
  $$ \mathcal{H}\{x_1[n]+x_2[n]\} = \mathcal{H}\{x_1[n]\} + \mathcal{H}\{x_2[n]\} $$
  $$ \mathcal{H}\{c\,x[n]\} = c\,\mathcal{H}\{x[n]\} $$
- Time invariance:
  $$ \mathcal{H}\{x[n-m]\} = y[n-m] $$
- Connection between impulse response and system output
