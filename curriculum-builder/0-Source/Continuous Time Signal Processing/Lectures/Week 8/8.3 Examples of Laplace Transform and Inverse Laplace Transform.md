## Lecture Outline (Fourier Transform Properties: Linearity, Shifted Impulses, and the Transform of a Cosine)

### 1. Review of the Fourier Transform
- The Fourier transform maps a time-domain signal to the frequency domain:
  $$ X(j\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- This is used to analyze signals in terms of their frequency content.

---

### 2. Why Fourier Transform Properties Matter
- Many signals are not given in a simple form.
- They may be:
  - scaled
  - shifted in time
  - shifted in frequency
  - added together
- Fourier transform properties let us simplify these problems instead of evaluating the integral from scratch every time.

---

### 3. Linearity Property
- If:
  $$ x_1(t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; X_1(j\omega) $$
  $$ x_2(t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; X_2(j\omega) $$
- Then:
  $$ A_1x_1(t)+A_2x_2(t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; A_1X_1(j\omega)+A_2X_2(j\omega) $$
- This follows directly from splitting the Fourier transform integral into two parts.

---

### 4. Fourier Transform of a Shifted Impulse in Time
- Consider the signal:
  $$ x(t)=A\delta(t-t_0) $$
- Applying the Fourier transform:
  $$ X(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} A\delta(t-t_0)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- Using the sifting property of the delta function:
  $$ X(j\omega)=Ae^{-j\omega t_0} $$
- Therefore, the transform pair is:
  $$ A\delta(t-t_0) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; Ae^{-j\omega t_0} $$

---

### 5. Shifted Impulse in the Frequency Domain
- Suppose the signal is given in the frequency domain as:
  $$ X(j\omega)=2\pi A\delta(\omega-\omega_0) $$
- Apply the inverse Fourier transform:
  $$ x(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} 2\pi A\delta(\omega-\omega_0)e^{j\omega t}\,d\omega $$
- Using the sifting property:
  $$ x(t)=Ae^{j\omega_0 t} $$
- So the transform pair is:
  $$ Ae^{j\omega_0 t} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; 2\pi A\delta(\omega-\omega_0) $$

---

### 6. Interpretation of the Previous Two Results
- A shifted impulse in time becomes a complex exponential in frequency.
- A shifted impulse in frequency becomes a complex exponential in time.
- These are dual ideas and are very useful in signal analysis.

---

### 7. Example: Fourier Transform of a Cosine
- Given:
  $$ x(t)=A\cos(\omega_0 t) $$
- Use Euler’s identity:
  $$ \cos(\omega_0 t)=\frac{1}{2}\left(e^{j\omega_0 t}+e^{-j\omega_0 t}\right) $$
- Therefore:
  $$ x(t)=\frac{A}{2}e^{j\omega_0 t}+\frac{A}{2}e^{-j\omega_0 t} $$

---

### 8. Apply Linearity and the Exponential Transform Pair
- From the earlier property:
  $$ \frac{A}{2}e^{j\omega_0 t} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; A\pi\delta(\omega-\omega_0) $$
- Similarly:
  $$ \frac{A}{2}e^{-j\omega_0 t} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; A\pi\delta(\omega+\omega_0) $$
- Adding them gives:
  $$ X(j\omega)=A\pi\delta(\omega-\omega_0)+A\pi\delta(\omega+\omega_0) $$
- Or equivalently:
  $$ X(j\omega)=A\pi\left[\delta(\omega-\omega_0)+\delta(\omega+\omega_0)\right] $$

---

### 9. Interpretation of the Cosine Spectrum
- A single cosine in the time domain becomes two impulses in the frequency domain:
  - one at:
    $$ \omega=\omega_0 $$
  - one at:
    $$ \omega=-\omega_0 $$
- This reflects the positive- and negative-frequency components of the cosine signal.

---

### 10. Main Takeaways
- The Fourier transform is linear:
  $$ A_1x_1(t)+A_2x_2(t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; A_1X_1(j\omega)+A_2X_2(j\omega) $$
- A shifted impulse in time transforms to:
  $$ A\delta(t-t_0) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; Ae^{-j\omega t_0} $$
- A shifted impulse in frequency transforms to:
  $$ 2\pi A\delta(\omega-\omega_0) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; Ae^{j\omega_0 t} $$
- A cosine transforms to two symmetric impulses:
  $$ A\cos(\omega_0 t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; A\pi\left[\delta(\omega-\omega_0)+\delta(\omega+\omega_0)\right] $$
