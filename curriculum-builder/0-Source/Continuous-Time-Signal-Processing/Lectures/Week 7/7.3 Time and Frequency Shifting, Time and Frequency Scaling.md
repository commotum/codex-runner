## Lecture Outline (Fourier Transform Properties: Time Shifting, Scaling, Time Reversal, and Frequency Shifting)

### 1. Why Fourier Transform Properties Matter
- In practice, signals are often:
  - shifted in time
  - stretched or compressed
  - multiplied by exponentials or cosines
- Rather than recomputing every Fourier transform from scratch, these properties let us reuse known transform pairs.

---

### 2. Time-Shifting Property
- If:
  $$ x(t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; X(j\omega) $$
- Then:
  $$ x(t-t_0) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; e^{-j\omega t_0}X(j\omega) $$

---

### 3. Derivation Idea for Time Shifting
- Start from:
  $$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t-t_0)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- Use the change of variable:
  $$ \tau = t-t_0 $$
- Then:
  $$ t=\tau+t_0, \qquad dt=d\tau $$
- This gives:
  $$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(\tau)e^{-j\omega(\tau+t_0)}\,d\tau
     = e^{-j\omega t_0}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(\tau)e^{-j\omega\tau}\,d\tau $$
- Therefore:
  $$ x(t-t_0) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; e^{-j\omega t_0}X(j\omega) $$

---

### 4. Interpretation of Time Shifting
- Shifting the signal in time does **not** change the magnitude spectrum.
- It only multiplies the spectrum by a phase factor:
  $$ e^{-j\omega t_0} $$
- So:
  - magnitude stays the same
  - phase changes linearly with $\omega$

---

### 5. Time-Scaling Property
- If:
  $$ x(t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; X(j\omega) $$
- Then:
  $$ x(at) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \frac{1}{|a|}X\!\left(j\frac{\omega}{a}\right) $$

---

### 6. Interpretation of Time Scaling
- If $a>1$:
  - the signal is compressed in time
  - the spectrum expands in frequency
- If $0<a<1$:
  - the signal is stretched in time
  - the spectrum compresses in frequency
- The amplitude is also scaled by:
  $$ \frac{1}{|a|} $$

---

### 7. Time Reversal as a Special Case
- Set:
  $$ a=-1 $$
- Then:
  $$ x(-t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; X(-j\omega) $$
- So time reversal flips the spectrum about $\omega=0$.

---

### 8. Example Intuition for Time Scaling
- A rectangular pulse of width $\tau$ has a sinc-shaped spectrum.
- If the pulse is expanded to width $2\tau$, the spectrum becomes narrower.
- So:
  - wider signal in time $\Rightarrow$ narrower spectrum in frequency
  - narrower signal in time $\Rightarrow$ wider spectrum in frequency

---

### 9. Frequency-Shifting Property
- If:
  $$ x(t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; X(j\omega) $$
- Then:
  $$ x(t)e^{j\omega_0 t} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; X\!\left(j(\omega-\omega_0)\right) $$
- Similarly:
  $$ x(t)e^{-j\omega_0 t} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; X\!\left(j(\omega+\omega_0)\right) $$

---

### 10. Derivation Idea for Frequency Shifting
- Start with:
  $$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)e^{j\omega_0 t}e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- Combine the exponentials:
  $$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-j(\omega-\omega_0)t}\,dt $$
- This is exactly:
  $$ X\!\left(j(\omega-\omega_0)\right) $$

---

### 11. Real-Valued Version Using Cosine Modulation
- Since:
  $$ \cos(\omega_0 t)=\frac{1}{2}e^{j\omega_0 t}+\frac{1}{2}e^{-j\omega_0 t} $$
- Then:
  $$ x(t)\cos(\omega_0 t)
     = \frac{1}{2}x(t)e^{j\omega_0 t} + \frac{1}{2}x(t)e^{-j\omega_0 t} $$
- Applying the frequency-shifting property:
  $$ x(t)\cos(\omega_0 t)
     \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
     \frac{1}{2}X\!\left(j(\omega-\omega_0)\right)
     + \frac{1}{2}X\!\left(j(\omega+\omega_0)\right) $$

---

### 12. Interpretation of Cosine Modulation
- Multiplying a signal by a cosine creates **two shifted copies** of the original spectrum:
  - one centered at $+\omega_0$
  - one centered at $-\omega_0$
- Each copy is scaled by:
  $$ \frac{1}{2} $$
- The bandwidth of each shifted copy remains the same as the original.

---

### 13. Bandwidth Picture
- Suppose the original signal has spectrum concentrated in:
  $$ -W \le \omega \le W $$
- After multiplying by $\cos(\omega_0 t)$, the spectrum is shifted to:
  $$ \omega_0-W \le \omega \le \omega_0+W $$
  and
  $$ -\omega_0-W \le \omega \le -\omega_0+W $$
- So the signal is translated in frequency without changing its basic shape.

---

### 14. Communication / Radio Application
- This frequency-shifting idea is the basis of carrier modulation.
- Different radio stations may have similar baseband signal shapes, but each multiplies its signal by a different carrier frequency:
  $$ \omega_{0,1},\; \omega_{0,2},\; \omega_{0,3},\dots $$
- That shifts each station’s spectrum to a different band.
- A receiver then tunes to the desired carrier frequency and isolates the desired station.

---

### 15. Why the Carrier Frequency Matters
- The carrier frequency moves the signal away from baseband and separates it from other signals.
- This prevents overlapping transmissions from different sources and makes practical communication possible.

---

### 16. Main Takeaways
- **Time shift**:
  $$ x(t-t_0) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; e^{-j\omega t_0}X(j\omega) $$
- **Time scaling**:
  $$ x(at) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \frac{1}{|a|}X\!\left(j\frac{\omega}{a}\right) $$
- **Time reversal**:
  $$ x(-t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; X(-j\omega) $$
- **Frequency shift**:
  $$ x(t)e^{j\omega_0 t} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; X\!\left(j(\omega-\omega_0)\right) $$
- **Cosine modulation**:
  $$ x(t)\cos(\omega_0 t)
     \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
     \frac{1}{2}X\!\left(j(\omega-\omega_0)\right)
     + \frac{1}{2}X\!\left(j(\omega+\omega_0)\right) $$

---

### 17. Big Picture
- Time-domain changes produce predictable frequency-domain changes.
- These properties are essential for:
  - signal analysis
  - filter design
  - modulation
  - communication systems
