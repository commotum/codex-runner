## Lecture Outline (Unit Step Function and Building Signals from It)

### 1. Important Functions as Signal Building Blocks
- The lecture introduces functions that can be used as **building blocks** for other signals.
- Main focus:
  - the **unit step function**
  - how it can control where a signal starts/stops
  - how it can be used to construct more complicated signals

---

### 2. Unit Step Function
- The unit step function is defined as:
  $$
  u(t)=
  \begin{cases}
  1, & t \ge 0 \\
  0, & t < 0
  \end{cases}
  $$
- It is **discontinuous** at $t=0$.
- Intuitive interpretation:
  - behaves like a **switch**
  - off for $t<0$
  - on for $t \ge 0$

---

### 3. Using the Unit Step to Control Another Signal
- Example exponential signal:
  $$
  x(t)=e^{-t}
  $$
- Multiplying by the unit step gives:
  $$
  f(t)=e^{-t}u(t)
  $$
- This forces the signal to be zero for negative time:
  $$
  f(t)=
  \begin{cases}
  0, & t<0 \\
  e^{-t}, & t \ge 0
  \end{cases}
  $$
- Key idea:
  - $u(t)$ lets us **control the starting point** of a signal

---

### 4. Building a Rectangular Pulse from Shifted Step Functions
- Suppose we want a rectangular signal that is equal to $1$ only from $t=2$ to $t=4$.
- Shifted unit steps:
  $$
  u(t-2), \qquad u(t-4)
  $$
- Then the rectangular pulse is:
  $$
  x(t)=u(t-2)-u(t-4)
  $$
- Interpretation:
  - $u(t-2)$ turns the signal on at $t=2$
  - $u(t-4)$ turns it off at $t=4$

---

### 5. Example: Constructing a Triangular Signal
- Desired signal:
  - rises linearly from $0$ to $2$ on $0 \le t \le 2$
  - then decreases linearly to $0$ on $2 \le t \le 3$
- Strategy:
  - break the signal into two simpler parts
  $$
  x(t)=x_1(t)+x_2(t)
  $$

---

### 6. First Part of the Triangle
- Use a ramp:
  $$
  f_1(t)=t
  $$
- Restrict it to the interval $0 \le t \le 2$ with a rectangular window:
  $$
  f_2(t)=u(t)-u(t-2)
  $$
- Then:
  $$
  x_1(t)=t[u(t)-u(t-2)]
  $$
- This creates the rising portion of the triangle.

---

### 7. Second Part of the Triangle
- The descending line has slope $-2$ and passes through $t=3$, so:
  $$
  f_1(t)=-2(t-3)
  $$
- Restrict it to the interval $2 \le t \le 3$:
  $$
  f_2(t)=u(t-2)-u(t-3)
  $$
- Then:
  $$
  x_2(t)=-2(t-3)[u(t-2)-u(t-3)]
  $$
- This creates the falling portion of the triangle.

---

### 8. Final Expression for the Triangular Signal
- Combine the two pieces:
  $$
  x(t)=t[u(t)-u(t-2)]-2(t-3)[u(t-2)-u(t-3)]
  $$
- After simplification:
  $$
  x(t)=t\,u(t)-3(t-2)u(t-2)+2(t-3)u(t-3)
  $$

---

### 9. Main Takeaways
- The unit step function acts like a **switch**.
- Shifted step functions can be combined to form **rectangular pulses**.
- Multiplying a signal by step functions can **restrict it to a specific time interval**.
- More complicated signals, like triangular signals, can be built from:
  - ramps
  - rectangular windows
  - unit step functions

---

### 10. Big Picture
- The unit step function is important because it serves as a **basic building block** for signal construction.
- Later, it can be used to model and analyze more complicated signals in a systematic way.
