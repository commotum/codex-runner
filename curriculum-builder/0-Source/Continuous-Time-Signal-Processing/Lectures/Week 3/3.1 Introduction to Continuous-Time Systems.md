## Lecture Outline (Continuous-Time Systems)

### 1. System as a Black Box
- A system can be viewed as a **black box** that takes an input signal and produces an output signal:
  $$ x(t) \longrightarrow \text{System} \longrightarrow y(t) $$
- Here:
  - $x(t)$ is the input signal
  - $y(t)$ is the output signal
- The output is generally a transformed or refined version of the input.

---

### 2. Formal Definition of a System
- A system is a **process** that transforms an input signal into an output signal.
- In this course, the signals are usually time-dependent, so:
  - input: $x(t)$
  - output: $y(t)$
- The main goal is to understand the relationship between $x(t)$ and $y(t)$.

---

### 3. Example 1: Filter System
- Input:
  - a noisy signal $x(t)$
- System:
  - a filter that removes unwanted noise, especially high-frequency noise
- Output:
  - a cleaner signal $y(t)$
- Interpretation:
  - the system processes the original signal and produces a refined version

---

### 4. Example 2: RC Circuit as a System
- Consider an RC circuit with:
  - source voltage $v_s(t)$
  - resistor $R$
  - capacitor $C$
- Input signal:
  $$ x(t) = v_s(t) $$
- Output signal:
  $$ y(t) = v_c(t) $$
  where $v_c(t)$ is the capacitor voltage

---

### 5. Current Relations in the RC Circuit
- By Ohm’s law, the current through the resistor is:
  $$ i(t) = \frac{v_s(t)-v_c(t)}{R} $$
- For the capacitor:
  $$ i(t) = C\frac{dv_c(t)}{dt} $$

---

### 6. Input–Output Differential Equation for the RC Circuit
- Set the two current expressions equal:
  $$ \frac{v_s(t)-v_c(t)}{R} = C\frac{dv_c(t)}{dt} $$
- Rearranging:
  $$ \frac{v_s(t)}{RC} = \frac{dv_c(t)}{dt} + \frac{v_c(t)}{RC} $$
- In input-output form:
  $$ \frac{dy(t)}{dt} + \frac{1}{RC}y(t) = \frac{1}{RC}x(t) $$
- If we define:
  $$ a = \frac{1}{RC} $$
  then:
  $$ \frac{dy(t)}{dt} + ay(t) = ax(t) $$
- This is a **first-order differential equation** describing the system.

---

### 7. Example 3: Automobile System
- Consider a car moving forward under:
  - applied force from the engine: $F_a(t)$
  - retarding force from friction / drag: $F_r(t)$
- The retarding force is modeled as:
  $$ F_r(t) = \rho v(t) $$
- Input signal:
  $$ x(t) = F_a(t) $$
- Output signal:
  $$ y(t) = v(t) $$
  where $v(t)$ is the car’s velocity

---

### 8. Input–Output Differential Equation for the Automobile
- Apply Newton’s second law:
  $$ \sum F = ma $$
- So:
  $$ F_a(t) - F_r(t) = m\frac{dv(t)}{dt} $$
- Substitute $F_r(t)=\rho v(t)$:
  $$ F_a(t) - \rho v(t) = m\frac{dv(t)}{dt} $$
- Rearranging:
  $$ \frac{dv(t)}{dt} + \frac{\rho}{m}v(t) = \frac{1}{m}F_a(t) $$
- In input-output form:
  $$ \frac{dy(t)}{dt} + by(t) = cx(t) $$
  where:
  $$ b=\frac{\rho}{m}, \qquad c=\frac{1}{m} $$

---

### 9. Example 4: Image Processing System
- Systems do not always have to process time signals.
- A system can also take a **2D image** as input.
- Example:
  - input: a blurry image
  - output: a sharpened image
- This shows that systems can act on spatial signals as well, not just time-domain signals.

---

### 10. Key Idea About Systems
- In every example, the system:
  - takes a signal as input
  - processes it
  - produces another signal as output
- The output is determined by the internal behavior of the system.

---

### 11. Main Takeaways
- A continuous-time system is modeled as:
  $$ x(t) \longrightarrow \text{System} \longrightarrow y(t) $$
- Systems can represent many physical processes, including:
  - filters
  - electrical circuits
  - automobiles
  - image-processing operations
- For physical systems like RC circuits and automobile motion, the input-output relationship is often described by a **first-order differential equation**.
