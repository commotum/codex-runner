## Lecture Outline (Frequency Response of LTI Systems)

### 1. Review of Convolution for LTI Systems
- For an LTI system, the output is given by convolution:
  $$ y(t) = x(t) * h(t) $$
- Equivalent integral form:
  $$ y(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(\tau)\,x(t-\tau)\,d\tau $$
- Here:
  - $x(t)$ is the input
  - $y(t)$ is the output
  - $h(t)$ is the impulse response

---

### 2. Meaning of the Impulse Response
- If the input to the LTI system is an impulse:
  $$ \delta(t) $$
- Then the output is:
  $$ h(t) $$
- So $h(t)$ completely characterizes the LTI system.
- Once $h(t)$ is known, the output for any input can be found using convolution.

---

### 3. Sinusoidal / Complex-Exponential Input
- The lecture studies the special input:
  $$ x(t) = A e^{j(\omega t + \phi)} $$
- This represents a sinusoidal signal in complex-exponential form.
- Goal:
  - determine the output of an LTI system when the input is a sinusoid / complex exponential

---

### 4. Substitute the Input into the Convolution Formula
- Start with:
  $$ y(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(\tau)\,x(t-\tau)\,d\tau $$
- Since:
  $$ x(t-\tau) = A e^{j(\omega (t-\tau) + \phi)} $$
- Substitute:
  $$ y(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(\tau)\,A e^{j(\omega (t-\tau)+\phi)}\,d\tau $$

---

### 5. Rearranging the Expression
- Split the exponential:
  $$ y(t) = A e^{j\phi} e^{j\omega t}
     \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(\tau)e^{-j\omega \tau}\,d\tau $$
- Recognize:
  $$ A e^{j\phi} e^{j\omega t} = x(t) $$
- So:
  $$ y(t) = H(j\omega)\,x(t) $$

---

### 6. Definition of Frequency Response
- The term
  $$ H(j\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(\tau)e^{-j\omega \tau}\,d\tau $$
  is called the **frequency response** of the LTI system.

---

### 7. Main Frequency-Response Result
- If the input to an LTI system is:
  $$ x(t)=A e^{j(\omega t+\phi)} $$
- Then the output is:
  $$ y(t)=H(j\omega)\,A e^{j(\omega t+\phi)} $$
- Interpretation:
  - the output is also a sinusoid / complex exponential
  - the **frequency stays the same**
  - only the **magnitude** and **phase** are changed by $H(j\omega)$

---

### 8. Interpretation of $H(j\omega)$
- Since $H(j\omega)$ is generally complex, it affects:
  - amplitude
  - phase
- If:
  $$ H(j\omega)=|H(j\omega)|e^{j\angle H(j\omega)} $$
- Then:
  $$ y(t)=|H(j\omega)|A\,e^{j(\omega t+\phi+\angle H(j\omega))} $$
- So:
  - output amplitude becomes:
    $$ A|H(j\omega)| $$
  - output phase becomes:
    $$ \phi+\angle H(j\omega) $$

---

### 9. Key Property of LTI Systems
- A sinusoidal input to an LTI system produces a sinusoidal output at the **same frequency**.
- The system only scales and phase-shifts that sinusoid.
- This is why sinusoidal signals are especially useful for analyzing LTI systems.

---

### 10. Frequency Response Formula vs. Fourier Transform
- The lecture points out that:
  $$ H(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- This has the same mathematical form as the Fourier transform:
  $$ X(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- Therefore:
  - the **frequency response is the Fourier transform of the impulse response**

---

### 11. Example: Exponential Impulse Response
- Suppose the impulse response is:
  $$ h(t)=e^{-t}u(t) $$
- The frequency response is:
  $$ H(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- Since $u(t)$ makes the signal one-sided:
  $$ H(j\omega)=\int_0^{\infty} e^{-t}e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$

---

### 12. Evaluate the Example Integral
- Combine the exponentials:
  $$ H(j\omega)=\int_0^{\infty} e^{-(1+j\omega)t}\,dt $$
- Evaluating the integral gives:
  $$ H(j\omega)=\frac{1}{1+j\omega} $$

---

### 13. Magnitude of the Frequency Response
- The magnitude is:
  $$ |H(j\omega)| = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\omega^2}} $$
- This decreases as $|\omega|$ increases.

---

### 14. Interpretation as a Low-Pass Filter
- Since:
  $$ |H(j\omega)| \approx 1 \quad \text{for small } \omega $$
  and
  $$ |H(j\omega)| \to 0 \quad \text{for large } |\omega| $$
- The system attenuates high-frequency signals more strongly than low-frequency signals.
- Therefore, this LTI system behaves like a **low-pass filter**.

---

### 15. Physical Interpretation of the Example
- If you apply a low-frequency sinusoid:
  - the output sinusoid is only slightly reduced
- If you apply a high-frequency sinusoid:
  - the output sinusoid is heavily attenuated
- In both cases, the output frequency remains the same as the input frequency.

---

### 16. Main Takeaways
- Convolution for an LTI system:
  $$ y(t)=x(t)*h(t) $$
- Frequency response:
  $$ H(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- For sinusoidal input:
  $$ y(t)=H(j\omega)x(t) $$
- An LTI system:
  - preserves sinusoidal frequency
  - changes only amplitude and phase
- Example:
  $$ h(t)=e^{-t}u(t)
     \;\Longrightarrow\;
     H(j\omega)=\frac{1}{1+j\omega} $$
- This example is a **low-pass filter** because high-frequency components are attenuated.
