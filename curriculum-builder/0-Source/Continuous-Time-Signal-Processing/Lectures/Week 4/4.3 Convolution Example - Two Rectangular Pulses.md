## Lecture Outline (Convolution of Rectangular Pulses)

### 1. Example Setup
- Two rectangular pulses are given:

  $$
  x(t)=
  \begin{cases}
  1, & 0 \le t \le 1 \\
  0, & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
  $$

  $$
  h(t)=
  \begin{cases}
  1, & 0 \le t \le 3 \\
  0, & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
  $$

- The goal is to compute their convolution:
  $$ y(t) = (x*h)(t) $$

---

### 2. Convolution Integral
- The continuous-time convolution formula is:

  $$
  (x*h)(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(\tau)\,h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
  $$

- Here:
  - $\tau$ is the variable of integration
  - $t$ is treated as fixed while evaluating the integral

---

### 3. Graphical Convolution Procedure
To compute $x(t)*h(t)$ graphically:

1. Rewrite the signals as functions of $\tau$:
   $$ x(\tau), \quad h(\tau) $$

2. Flip $h(\tau)$ about $\tau=0$:
   $$ h(-\tau) $$

3. Shift the flipped signal by $t$:
   $$ h(t-\tau) $$

4. Multiply:
   $$ x(\tau)\,h(t-\tau) $$

5. Integrate the product over all $\tau$:
   $$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(\tau)\,h(t-\tau)\,d\tau $$

- Geometrically, this is the **area of overlap** between $x(\tau)$ and $h(t-\tau)$.

---

### 4. Support of the Shifted Pulse
- Since $h(t)$ is nonzero on $[0,3]$, the flipped-and-shifted version $h(t-\tau)$ is nonzero on:
  $$ t-3 \le \tau \le t $$
- Since $x(\tau)$ is nonzero on:
  $$ 0 \le \tau \le 1 $$
- The convolution depends entirely on how these two intervals overlap.

---

### 5. Example at $t=\frac{1}{2}$
- For:
  $$ t=\frac{1}{2} $$
- The shifted pulse overlaps $x(\tau)$ only on:
  $$ 0 \le \tau \le \frac{1}{2} $$
- Over that interval, both pulses have height $1$, so:
  $$ y\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2} $$

---

### 6. Case 1: $t<0$
- The shifted pulse lies completely to the left of $x(\tau)$.
- There is no overlap.
- Therefore:
  $$ y(t)=0, \quad t<0 $$

---

### 7. Case 2: $0 \le t \le 1$
- The overlap runs from:
  $$ 0 \le \tau \le t $$
- The overlap width is:
  $$ t $$
- Since both heights are $1$, the area is:
  $$ y(t)=t, \quad 0 \le t \le 1 $$

---

### 8. Case 3: $1 \le t \le 3$
- The shifted pulse fully covers the support of $x(\tau)$.
- The overlap is the full interval:
  $$ 0 \le \tau \le 1 $$
- So the overlap width is:
  $$ 1 $$
- Therefore:
  $$ y(t)=1, \quad 1 \le t \le 3 $$

---

### 9. Case 4: $3 \le t \le 4$
- The shifted pulse moves to the right and only partially overlaps $x(\tau)$.
- The overlap interval is:
  $$ t-3 \le \tau \le 1 $$
- The overlap width is:
  $$ 1-(t-3)=4-t $$
- Therefore:
  $$ y(t)=4-t, \quad 3 \le t \le 4 $$

---

### 10. Case 5: $t>4$
- The shifted pulse lies completely to the right of $x(\tau)$.
- There is no overlap.
- Therefore:
  $$ y(t)=0, \quad t>4 $$

---

### 11. Final Piecewise Answer
- Collecting all cases:

  $$
  (x*h)(t)=
  \begin{cases}
  0, & t<0 \\
  t, & 0 \le t \le 1 \\
  1, & 1 \le t \le 3 \\
  4-t, & 3 \le t \le 4 \\
  0, & t>4
  \end{cases}
  $$

---

### 12. Shape of the Result
- The convolution of these two rectangular pulses is a **trapezoid**:
  - rises linearly from $0$ to $1$
  - stays flat at height $1$
  - then decreases linearly back to $0$

---

### 13. General Observations from the Example
- Convolving two rectangular pulses produces:
  - a **trapezoid** if the widths are different
  - a **triangle** if the widths are equal
- The width of the flat region is the difference in pulse widths:
  $$ 3-1=2 $$
- The slopes of the rising and falling edges depend on the amplitudes of the pulses.

---

### 14. Main Takeaway
- Convolution can be interpreted as a moving overlap calculation.
- For rectangular pulses, the output is determined entirely by the changing overlap area between the two signals as one slides across the other.
