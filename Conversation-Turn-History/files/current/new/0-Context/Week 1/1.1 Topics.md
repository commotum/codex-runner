# 1.1 Topics

1. **Recognizing Periodic Continuous-Time Signals** - determine whether a continuous-time signal is periodic and find its fundamental period.
   Core move: apply the periodicity condition to identify a repeating interval and decide whether it is fundamental.
   Prerequisite math topics:
   - Function notation and interpreting expressions like `x(t)`.
   - Integers and integer multiples.
   - Reading repeating structure from a graph or interval description.
   Pre-existing MA prerequisites:
   - `Introduction to Functions`
   - `Visual Representations of Functions`
   - `Graphs of Functions`
   - `Periodic Functions`
   This comes from sections 1-2.

2. **Reading Parameters of Real-Valued Sinusoids** - read the amplitude, angular frequency, period, and phase of a sinusoid.
   Core move: extract the parameters of a sinusoid from its standard form and connect angular frequency to period.
   Prerequisite math topics:
   - Basic sine and cosine functions.
   - Radian measure and working with `pi`.
   - Solving simple equations such as `omega_0 = 2pi / T`.
   - Reading coefficients and constants from an algebraic expression.
   Pre-existing MA prerequisites:
   - `Introduction to Radians`
   - `Trigonometric Ratios With Radians`
   - `Graphing Sine and Cosine`
   - `Describing Properties of the Sine Function`
   - `Describing Properties of the Cosine Function`
   - `Horizontal Translations of Trigonometric Functions`
   - `Properties of Transformed Sine and Cosine Functions`
   - `Constructing Algebraic Expressions`
   - `Evaluating Linear Expressions`
   - `Identifying Coefficients and Constants`
   This comes from sections 3-5.
   The physical examples belong here as support, not as their own lesson.

3. **Identifying Harmonics from Fundamental Frequency** - determine whether a sinusoid is a harmonic of a given fundamental frequency.
   Core move: compare a sinusoid's frequency to a fundamental frequency and identify the harmonic number.
   Prerequisite math topics:
   - Integer multiples and divisibility.
   - Ratios and proportional reasoning.
   - Radian measure with `pi`-based frequencies.
   - Converting between angular frequency and period.
   Pre-existing MA prerequisites:
   - `Introduction to Ratios`
   - `Equivalent Ratios`
   - `Writing Ratios Using Fractions`
   - `Reasoning With Equivalent Ratios`
   - `Further Reasoning With Equivalent Ratios`
   - `Introduction to Radians`
   - `Trigonometric Ratios With Radians`
   - `Graphing Sine and Cosine`
   - `Describing Properties of the Sine Function`
   - `Describing Properties of the Cosine Function`
   This comes from sections 6-8.

4. **Determining When Sums Stay Periodic** - determine whether a sum of periodic signals is periodic from their period ratio.
   Core move: test whether two periods have a common multiple by checking whether their ratio is rational.
   Prerequisite math topics:
   - Ratios and rational numbers.
   - Integer multiples and common multiples.
   - Solving simple algebraic relations involving two periods.
   - Equivalent fractions and proportional equivalence.
   Pre-existing MA prerequisites:
   - `Least Common Multiples`
   - `Introduction to Ratios`
   - `Equivalent Ratios`
   - `Writing Ratios Using Fractions`
   - `Reasoning With Equivalent Ratios`
   - `Further Reasoning With Equivalent Ratios`
   - `Periodic Functions`
   This is section 9, with section 10 partly serving as setup.

5. **Applying Product-to-Sum to Signal Products** - rewrite a product of sinusoids to identify the frequencies it contains.
   Core move: use the product-to-sum identity to convert a product into sinusoidal components and read off the resulting frequencies.
   Prerequisite math topics:
   - Basic sine and cosine notation.
   - Substituting values into an algebraic identity.
   - Simplifying algebraic expressions with trigonometric terms.
   - Reading frequency from expressions like `cos(k t)`.
   Pre-existing MA prerequisites:
   - `Introduction to Radians`
   - `Trigonometric Ratios With Radians`
   - `Graphing Sine and Cosine`
   - `Describing Properties of the Sine Function`
   - `Describing Properties of the Cosine Function`
   - `The Sum and Difference Formulas for Sine`
   - `The Sum and Difference Formulas for Cosine`
   - `Writing Sums of Trigonometric Functions in Amplitude-Phase Form`
   - `The Double-Angle Formula for Cosine`
   This comes from sections 10-13.

Section 14 should not become its own lesson. It is a summary.
