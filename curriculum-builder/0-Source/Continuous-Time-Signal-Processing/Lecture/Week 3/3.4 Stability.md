## Lecture Outline (Stability and BIBO Stability)

### 1. Systems and Input–Output View
- A system takes an input signal $x(t)$ and produces an output signal $y(t)$:
  $$ y(t)=\mathcal{T}\{x(t)\} $$
- The input–output relationship can often be expressed with a differential equation.

---

### 2. Definition of Stability
- A system is **stable** if every bounded input produces a bounded output.
- This is called **BIBO stability**:
  - **B**ounded **I**nput
  - **B**ounded **O**utput

---

### 3. Mathematical Description of Bounded Signals
- A bounded input satisfies:
  $$ |x(t)| \le M, \quad \forall t $$
- A bounded output satisfies:
  $$ |y(t)| \le R, \quad \forall t $$
- If bounded input implies bounded output, the system is stable.

---

### 4. Intuition for Stability
- Stability means the output does **not blow up** when the input stays within finite limits.
- The lecture emphasizes that damping or dissipation is often what keeps a system stable.

---

### 5. Example: Pendulum vs. Inverted Pendulum
- Ordinary pendulum:
  - input: applied force $f(t)$
  - output: angular displacement $\theta(t)$
- A small disturbance causes oscillation, but the motion remains bounded.
- This is an example of a **stable** system.

- Inverted pendulum:
  - input: applied force $f(t)$
  - output: angular displacement $\theta(t)$
- Even a small disturbance can make the system fall away from equilibrium.
- This is an example of an **unstable** system.

---

### 6. RC Circuit as a Stable System
- Circuit:
  - input: source voltage $v_s(t)$
  - output: capacitor voltage $v_c(t)$
- The resistor dissipates energy:
  $$ p_R(t)=i^2(t)R $$
- Because $R>0$, the circuit has damping.
- This prevents the output from becoming unbounded.
- Therefore, the RC circuit is presented as a **stable** system.

---

### 7. Automobile System as a Stable System
- Input:
  $$ x(t)=F_a(t) $$
  where $F_a(t)$ is the applied force from the engine
- Output:
  $$ y(t)=v(t) $$
  where $v(t)$ is the velocity of the car
- Retarding force:
  $$ F_r(t)=\rho v(t) $$

---

### 8. Differential Equation for the Automobile
- Using Newton’s second law:
  $$ F_a(t)-F_r(t)=m\frac{dv(t)}{dt} $$
- Substitute $F_r(t)=\rho v(t)$:
  $$ F_a(t)-\rho v(t)=m\frac{dv(t)}{dt} $$
- Rearranging:
  $$ \frac{dv(t)}{dt}+\frac{\rho}{m}v(t)=\frac{1}{m}F_a(t) $$

---

### 9. Why the Automobile System Is Stable
- As velocity increases, the retarding force also increases:
  $$ F_r(t)=\rho v(t) $$
- Eventually, the retarding force balances the applied force:
  $$ F_a(t)=F_r(t) $$
- Then:
  $$ \frac{dv(t)}{dt}=0 $$
- So the car reaches a constant or terminal velocity:
  $$ v=\frac{F}{\rho} $$
- This retarding-force effect keeps the velocity bounded, so the system is stable.

---

### 10. Key Stability Insight from the First Examples
- The lecture highlights that **damping** or **energy dissipation** is often what keeps a system stable.
- In the examples:
  - the resistor provides damping in the RC circuit
  - the drag / retarding force provides damping in the automobile model

---

### 11. Example: Inductor System with $R=0$
- Consider a source and inductor, with:
  $$ R=0 $$
- Input:
  $$ x(t)=v_s(t) $$
- Output:
  $$ y(t)=i(t) $$
- Inductor relation:
  $$ v_l(t)=L\frac{di(t)}{dt} $$
- Since $R=0$, the source voltage appears entirely across the inductor:
  $$ v_s(t)=v_l(t) $$
- Therefore:
  $$ i(t)=\frac{1}{L}\int_{-\infty}^{t} v_s(\tau)\,d\tau $$

---

### 12. Step Input Case: Unstable Response
- Choose the bounded input:
  $$ v_s(t)=u(t) $$
- Then:
  $$ i(t)=\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{t} 1\,d\tau = \frac{t}{L} $$
- The input is bounded, but the output grows without bound.
- This violates BIBO stability.
- So this system is **not stable**.

---

### 13. Exponential Input Case: Bounded Response
- Choose another bounded input:
  $$ v_s(t)=e^{-at}u(t), \quad a>0 $$
- Then:
  $$ i(t)=\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{t} e^{-a\tau}\,d\tau $$
- Evaluating the integral:
  $$ i(t)=\frac{1}{aL}\left(1-e^{-at}\right) $$
- This output remains bounded.

---

### 14. Lecture’s “Marginally Stable” Description
- For the same $R=0$ inductor system:
  - a bounded step input gives an unbounded output
  - a bounded decaying exponential gives a bounded output
- The lecture describes this behavior as **marginally stable**.
- In BIBO terms, it is still **not stable**, because one bounded input already produces an unbounded output.

---

### 15. Main Takeaways
- BIBO stability means:
  $$ |x(t)| \le M \Rightarrow |y(t)| \le R $$
- Stable examples from the lecture:
  - ordinary pendulum
  - RC circuit with $R>0$
  - automobile with retarding force
- Unstable examples from the lecture:
  - inverted pendulum
  - ideal inductor system with $R=0$ under step input
- Core intuition:
  - damping and dissipation help keep outputs bounded
  - without damping, a system can accumulate energy and blow up
