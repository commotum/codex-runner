## Lecture Outline (Fourier Series Properties: Time Reversal, Time Scaling, and Conjugate Symmetry)

### 1. Review of the Complex Fourier Series
- A periodic signal can be written in complex exponential form as:
  $$ x(t) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} a_k e^{jk\omega_0 t} $$
- The Fourier series coefficients are:
  $$ a_k = \frac{1}{T}\int_{\text{one period}} x(t)e^{-jk\omega_0 t}\,dt $$
- Here:
  - $T$ is the period
  - $\omega_0 = \frac{2\pi}{T}$ is the fundamental frequency
  - $a_k$ are the complex Fourier coefficients

---

### 2. Why Fourier Series Properties Matter
- These properties let you solve new Fourier series problems without recalculating coefficients from scratch.
- Instead of recomputing integrals each time, you can transform known results using:
  - time reversal
  - time scaling
  - conjugate symmetry

---

### 3. Time Reversal Property
- Start with:
  $$ x(t) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} a_k e^{jk\omega_0 t} $$
- Replace $t$ by $-t$:
  $$ x(-t) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} a_k e^{jk\omega_0(-t)} $$
- Rewrite by changing the index $k \to -k$:
  $$ x(-t) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} a_{-k} e^{jk\omega_0 t} $$
- Therefore, the Fourier coefficients of $x(-t)$ are:
  $$ x(-t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; a_{-k} $$

---

### 4. Interpretation of Time Reversal
- Time reversal flips the signal in time.
- In the Fourier series domain, this corresponds to flipping the coefficient sequence:
  $$ a_k \to a_{-k} $$

---

### 5. Time Scaling Property
- Start again from:
  $$ x(t) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} a_k e^{jk\omega_0 t} $$
- Replace $t$ by $\alpha t$:
  $$ x(\alpha t) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} a_k e^{jk\omega_0(\alpha t)} $$
- Rearranging:
  $$ x(\alpha t) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} a_k e^{jk(\alpha\omega_0)t} $$

---

### 6. Effect of Time Scaling
- The Fourier coefficients do **not** change:
  $$ a_k \text{ remains the same} $$
- The fundamental frequency scales by $\alpha$:
  $$ \omega_0 \to \alpha\omega_0 $$
- So time scaling changes the frequency spacing of the harmonics, but not their weights.

---

### 7. Interpretation of Time Scaling
- Compressing or stretching the signal in time changes the frequencies of the sinusoidal components.
- The harmonic coefficients stay the same, but the harmonics occur at:
  $$ k(\alpha\omega_0) $$

---

### 8. Conjugate Symmetry Property
- For a real-valued periodic signal:
  $$ x(t) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} c_k e^{jk\omega_0 t} $$
- The Fourier coefficients are:
  $$ c_k = \frac{1}{T}\int_{\text{one period}} x(t)e^{-jk\omega_0 t}\,dt $$

---

### 9. Derivation of Conjugate Symmetry
- Compute $c_{-k}$:
  $$ c_{-k} = \frac{1}{T}\int_{\text{one period}} x(t)e^{jk\omega_0 t}\,dt $$
- Now take the complex conjugate of $c_k$:
  $$ c_k^* = \left(\frac{1}{T}\int_{\text{one period}} x(t)e^{-jk\omega_0 t}\,dt\right)^* $$
- Since $x(t)$ is real:
  $$ x^*(t) = x(t) $$
- And:
  $$ \left(e^{-jk\omega_0 t}\right)^* = e^{jk\omega_0 t} $$
- Therefore:
  $$ c_k^* = \frac{1}{T}\int_{\text{one period}} x(t)e^{jk\omega_0 t}\,dt = c_{-k} $$

---

### 10. Final Conjugate Symmetry Result
- For real-valued $x(t)$:
  $$ c_{-k} = c_k^* $$

---

### 11. Consequences of Conjugate Symmetry
- The magnitudes are symmetric:
  $$ |c_{-k}| = |c_k| $$
- The phases are opposite:
  $$ \angle c_{-k} = -\angle c_k $$
- So if one coefficient is known, the matching negative-index coefficient can be found immediately by complex conjugation.

---

### 12. Practical Use of Conjugate Symmetry
- Example idea:
  - if
    $$ c_2 = 2e^{-j45^\circ} $$
  - then automatically:
    $$ c_{-2} = 2e^{j45^\circ} $$
- This saves work when plotting or computing Fourier coefficients.

---

### 13. Connection to the Spectrum
- The coefficient sequence can be viewed as a spectrum indexed by $k$.
- Time reversal flips the spectrum:
  $$ a_k \to a_{-k} $$
- Time scaling changes the harmonic spacing:
  $$ \omega_0 \to \alpha\omega_0 $$
- Conjugate symmetry tells us how positive and negative harmonics relate when the signal is real-valued.

---

### 14. Main Takeaways
- If
  $$ x(t) \Longleftrightarrow a_k $$
  then:
  - **Time reversal**:
    $$ x(-t) \Longleftrightarrow a_{-k} $$
  - **Time scaling**:
    $$ x(\alpha t) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} a_k e^{jk(\alpha\omega_0)t} $$
  - **Conjugate symmetry for real signals**:
    $$ c_{-k} = c_k^* $$

---

### 15. Why These Properties Are Useful
- They let you reuse known Fourier series results.
- They reduce repeated integration.
- They make spectrum sketches and coefficient calculations much faster.
