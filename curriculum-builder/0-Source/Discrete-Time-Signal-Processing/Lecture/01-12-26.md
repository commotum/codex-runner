## Lecture Outline

### 1. Source Localization and Signal Propagation
- Source signal:
  $$ s(t) $$
- Received signals at multiple antennas:
  $$ r_1(t) = \alpha_1 s(t - D_1) $$
  $$ r_2(t) = \alpha_2 s(t - D_2) $$
  $$ r_3(t) = \alpha_3 s(t - D_3) $$
- Propagation delays and relative delay:
  $$ \tau_{12} = D_2 - D_1 $$
- Interpretation of delays for estimating source location

---

### 2. Discrete-Time Correlation
- Discrete-time received signals:
  $$ r_1[n], \; r_2[n] $$
- Cross-correlation definition:
  $$ \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} r_1[n]\, r_2[n-m] $$
- Use of correlation to estimate relative delay between signals

---

### 3. Sampling and Sampling Rate
- Sampling period:
  $$ T $$
- Sampling frequency:
  $$ F_s = \frac{1}{T} \;\text{samples/second} $$
- Relationship between continuous-time and discrete-time signals:
  $$ x[n] = x(t)\big|_{t = nT} $$

---

### 4. Continuous-Time Complex Exponentials
- Continuous-time complex exponential:
  $$ x(t) = A e^{j\Omega_0 t} $$
- Periodicity condition:
  $$ e^{j\Omega_0(t+p)} = e^{j\Omega_0 t} $$
- Resulting period:
  $$ p = \frac{2\pi}{\Omega_0} $$
- Frequency relationship:
  $$ f_0 = \frac{\Omega_0}{2\pi} $$

---

### 5. Discrete-Time Complex Exponentials
- Sampled exponential:
  $$ x[n] = A e^{j\Omega_0 T n} $$
- Periodicity condition:
  $$ x[n] = x[n+M] $$
- Constraint:
  $$ e^{j\Omega_0 T M} = 1 $$
- Resulting period:
  $$ M = \frac{2\pi}{\Omega_0 T} $$
- Note: $M$ may not be an integer

---

### 6. Digital (Normalized) Frequency
- Definition:
  $$ \omega_0 = \Omega_0 T $$
- Discrete-time signal representation:
  $$ x[n] = A e^{j\omega_0 n} $$
- Frequency equivalence:
  $$ e^{j(\omega_0 + 2\pi)n} = e^{j\omega_0 n} $$
- Discrete-time frequency is periodic with period:
  $$ 2\pi \;\text{radians/sample} $$

---

### 7. Frequency-Domain Periodicity
- Continuous-time frequency periodicity:
  $$ \text{Period} = \frac{2\pi}{T} \;\text{rad/s} $$
- Discrete-time frequency periodicity:
  $$ \text{Period} = 2\pi \;\text{rad/sample} $$
- Fundamental frequency interval:
  $$ (-\pi,\pi) \;\text{rad/sample} $$
  $$ \left(-\frac{\pi}{T}, \frac{\pi}{T}\right) \;\text{rad/s} $$

---

### 8. Sampling Theorem
- If $x(t)$ is bandlimited to:
  $$ \left(-\frac{\pi}{T}, \frac{\pi}{T}\right) $$
- Then $x(t)$ can be perfectly reconstructed from its samples

---

### 9. Euler’s Identity and Sinusoids
- Euler’s identity:
  $$ e^{j\omega n} = \cos(\omega n) + j\sin(\omega n) $$
  $$ e^{-j\omega n} = \cos(\omega n) - j\sin(\omega n) $$
- Cosine representation:
  $$ \cos(\omega n) = \frac{e^{j\omega n} + e^{-j\omega n}}{2} $$
