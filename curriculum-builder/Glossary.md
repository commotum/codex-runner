**course** – a bounded body of study within a larger discipline, treated as one coherent whole. It pulls together many related ideas, methods, and problem types under a single identity. In that sense, a course is a compressed label for a substantial region of a subject.

**unit** – a major conceptual region within a course. It groups together material that belongs to the same broad theme, mode of reasoning, or family of problems. A unit is large enough to have its own internal progression, but still clearly part of the larger whole.

**module** – a focused branch within a unit. It gathers ideas that are tightly connected by method, dependency, or purpose. Where a unit names a major territory, a module names one distinct thread inside that territory.

**topic** – an individual idea, method, tool, or pattern of reasoning that can be studied directly. It is specific enough to explain, practice, and assess on its own. This is usually the level where the material becomes concrete.
