## Lecture Outline (DTFT Definition, Inverse DTFT, and Basic Properties)

### 1. Discrete-Time Fourier Transform (DTFT)
- Definition:
  $$ X(e^{j\omega}) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} x[n]e^{-j\omega n} $$
- Transform notation:
  $$ x[n] \;\Longleftrightarrow\; X(e^{j\omega}) $$

---

### 2. Inverse DTFT
- Inverse formula:
  $$ x[n] = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} X(e^{j\omega})e^{j\omega n}\,d\omega $$

---

### 3. Verification of the Inverse DTFT Formula
- Substitute the DTFT into the inverse:
  $$
  \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
  \left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} x[k]e^{-j\omega k}\right)e^{j\omega n}\,d\omega
  $$
- Rearranging:
  $$
  \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} x[k]
  \left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} e^{j\omega(n-k)}\,d\omega\right)
  $$
- Orthogonality result:
  $$
  \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} e^{j\omega(n-k)}\,d\omega =
  \begin{cases}
  1, & n=k \\
  0, & n\ne k
  \end{cases}
  $$
- Therefore:
  $$ x[n] = x[n] $$

---

### 4. Example: DTFT of a Finite-Length Rectangular Sequence
- Sequence:
  $$
  x[n] =
  \begin{cases}
  1, & |n|\le N \\
  0, & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
  $$
- DTFT:
  $$ X(e^{j\omega}) = \sum_{n=-N}^{N} e^{-j\omega n} $$
- Rewrite as a geometric series:
  $$ X(e^{j\omega}) = e^{j\omega N}\sum_{n=0}^{2N} e^{-j\omega n} $$
- Closed-form result:
  $$ X(e^{j\omega}) = \frac{\sin\!\left(\frac{\omega}{2}(2N+1)\right)}{\sin\!\left(\frac{\omega}{2}\right)} $$

---

### 5. Periodicity of the DTFT
- Show that:
  $$ X(e^{j(\omega+2\pi)}) = X(e^{j\omega}) $$
- Conclusion:
  - The DTFT is periodic in $\omega$
  - Fundamental period:
    $$ 2\pi $$

---

### 6. Interpretation of the Inverse DTFT
- Reconstruction formula:
  $$ x[n] = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} X(e^{j\omega})e^{j\omega n}\,d\omega $$
- Interpretation:
  - $x[n]$ can be viewed as a superposition of many complex sinusoids $e^{j\omega n}$

---

### 7. Linearity Property
- Fourier transform is linear:
  $$ \mathcal{F}\{\alpha x_1[n] + \beta x_2[n]\}
     = \alpha \mathcal{F}\{x_1[n]\} + \beta \mathcal{F}\{x_2[n]\} $$
- Equivalently:
  $$ \mathcal{F}\{\alpha x_1[n] + \beta x_2[n]\}
     = \alpha X_1(e^{j\omega}) + \beta X_2(e^{j\omega}) $$

---

### 8. Time-Shift Property
- If:
  $$ x[n] \;\Longleftrightarrow\; X(e^{j\omega}) $$
- Then:
  $$ x[n-m] \;\Longleftrightarrow\; e^{-j\omega m}X(e^{j\omega}) $$
- Interpretation:
  - A time delay changes only the phase of the DTFT

---

### 9. Modulation Property
- If:
  $$ x[n] \;\Longleftrightarrow\; X(e^{j\omega}) $$
- Then:
  $$ x[n]e^{j\omega_0 n} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; X(e^{j(\omega-\omega_0)}) $$
- Interpretation:
  - Multiplication by a complex exponential in time shifts the spectrum in frequency

---

### 10. Symmetry for Real-Valued Signals
- For real-valued $x[n]$:
  - $X(e^{j\omega})$ is generally complex
  - It satisfies conjugate symmetry:
    $$ X(e^{j\omega}) = X^*(e^{-j\omega}) $$
