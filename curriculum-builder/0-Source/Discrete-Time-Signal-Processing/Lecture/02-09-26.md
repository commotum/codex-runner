## Lecture Outline (FIR Implementation, Frequency Response, Causality, and Stability)

### 1. FIR Convolution Structure
- LTI system:
  $$ y[n] = \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} h[m]\,x[n-m] $$
- If:
  $$ h[n] \neq 0 \text{ only for } n=0,1,\dots,N-1 $$
- Then:
  $$ y[n] = \sum_{m=0}^{N-1} h[m]\,x[n-m] $$
- Vector form (length $N=4$ example):
  $$
  H =
  \begin{bmatrix}
  h[0]\\h[1]\\h[2]\\h[3]
  \end{bmatrix},
  \quad
  X =
  \begin{bmatrix}
  x[n]\\x[n-1]\\x[n-2]\\x[n-3]
  \end{bmatrix}
  $$
- Output:
  $$ y[n] = H^T X $$

---

### 2. Tapped Delay Line Implementation
- Realization using delays and multipliers
- Update rule:
  $$
  X =
  \begin{bmatrix}
  x[n+1]\\
  x[n]\\
  x[n-1]\\
  x[n-2]
  \end{bmatrix}
  $$
- Efficient implementation by shifting stored samples

---

### 3. Example FIR Filters
#### (a) Noncausal Symmetric Filter
- Impulse response:
  $$
  h[n] =
  \begin{cases}
  0.5, & n=-1 \\
  1, & n=0 \\
  0.5, & n=1 \\
  0, & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
  $$
- Output:
  $$ y[n] = 0.5x[n+1] + x[n] + 0.5x[n-1] $$

#### (b) Causal Version (Delayed)
- Shift by one sample:
  $$ \tilde{y}[n] = y[n-1] $$
- Equivalent impulse response:
  $$ \tilde{h}[n] = 0.5\delta[n] + 1\delta[n-1] + 0.5\delta[n-2] $$
- Output:
  $$ \tilde{y}[n] = 0.5x[n] + x[n-1] + 0.5x[n-2] $$

---

### 4. Frequency Response of FIR Filters
- Definition:
  $$ H(e^{j\omega}) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} h[n]e^{-j\omega n} $$
- Example:
  $$ H(e^{j\omega}) = 0.5e^{j\omega} + 1 + 0.5e^{-j\omega} $$
- Delayed (causal) version:
  $$ \tilde{H}(e^{j\omega}) = H(e^{j\omega})e^{-j\omega} $$

---

### 5. Time Shift Property
- If:
  $$ h[n-m] $$
- Then frequency response:
  $$ \mathcal{F}\{h[n-m]\} = H(e^{j\omega})e^{-j\omega m} $$
- Time shift in impulse response → phase shift in frequency response

---

### 6. Cascaded Systems
- Cascade with delay:
  $$ h[n] * \delta[n-k] = h[n-k] $$
- Impulse response of cascade equals convolution of impulse responses

---

### 7. Bounded-Input Bounded-Output (BIBO) Stability
- Bounded input:
  $$ |x[n]| \le M_x < \infty $$
- System is BIBO stable if:
  $$ |y[n]| \le M_y < \infty $$

---

### 8. Stability Example (Nonlinear System)
- System:
  $$ y[n] = e^{x^2[n]} $$
- If $|x[n]| \le M_x$:
  $$ |y[n]| = e^{x^2[n]} \le e^{M_x^2} < \infty $$
- Therefore: BIBO stable

---

### 9. LTI System Stability Condition
- LTI system:
  $$ y[n] = \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} h[m]\,x[n-m] $$
- BIBO stability ⇔ impulse response is absolutely summable:
  $$ \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} |h[n]| < \infty $$
- Equivalently:
  $$ h[n] \in \ell^1 $$

---

### 10. Example: Accumulator System
- Impulse response:
  $$ h[n] = u[n] $$
- Output:
  $$ y[n] = \sum_{m=-\infty}^{n} x[m] $$
- Since:
  $$ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} |u[n]| = \infty $$
- Not absolutely summable ⇒ **not BIBO stable**
