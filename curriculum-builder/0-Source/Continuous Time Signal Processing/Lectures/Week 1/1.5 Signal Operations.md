## Lecture Outline (Signal Operations: Time Shifting, Time Scaling, and Time Reversal)

### 1. Main Signal Operations
This lecture introduces three basic signal operations:

- **Time shifting**
- **Time scaling**
- **Time reversal**

These operations are fundamental in signal processing because they change **when** a signal occurs, **how fast** it evolves, or **in which time direction** it is viewed.

---

### 2. Time Shifting
Time shifting moves a signal left or right along the time axis.

#### Delay
- Delaying a signal by $T>0$ seconds gives:
  $$ x(t-T) $$
- This shifts the signal to the **right**.

#### Advance
- Advancing a signal by $T>0$ seconds gives:
  $$ x(t+T) $$
- This shifts the signal to the **left**.

#### Sign Interpretation
- In the form $x(t-T)$:
  - if $T>0$, the signal is **delayed**
  - if $T<0$, the signal is **advanced**

---

### 3. Physical Interpretation of Time Shifting
A radar-transmitted signal may reach two receivers at different times.

- A receiver closer to the transmitter receives the signal earlier.
- A receiver farther away receives a **delayed** version of the same signal.

So one received waveform may be a shifted version of another.

---

### 4. Example of Time Shifting
Given:
$$
x(t)=
\begin{cases}
e^{-2t}, & t \ge 0 \\
0, & t<0
\end{cases}
$$

This is a decaying exponential that starts at $t=0$.

#### Delay by 1 second
$$
x_d(t)=x(t-1)=
\begin{cases}
e^{-2(t-1)}, & t \ge 1 \\
0, & t<1
\end{cases}
$$

- The signal starts at $t=1$ instead of $t=0$.

#### Advance by 1 second
$$
x_a(t)=x(t+1)=
\begin{cases}
e^{-2(t+1)}, & t \ge -1 \\
0, & t<-1
\end{cases}
$$

- The signal starts at $t=-1$ instead of $t=0$.

---

### 5. Time Scaling
Time scaling changes how fast or slow a signal evolves.

- **Compression** makes the signal happen faster.
- **Expansion** makes the signal happen slower.

#### Compression
- Replacing $t$ by $at$ with $a>1$ compresses the signal:
  $$ x(at) $$
- The signal becomes **faster**.

#### Expansion
- Replacing $t$ by $t/a$ with $a>1$ expands the signal:
  $$ x\left(\frac{t}{a}\right) $$
- The signal becomes **slower**.

---

### 6. Example of Time Scaling with Key Time Points
Suppose a signal has important time locations at $T_1$ and $T_2$.

#### Faster by a factor of 2
$$ x(2t) $$
- The key points move to:
  $$ \frac{T_1}{2}, \quad \frac{T_2}{2} $$

#### Slower by a factor of 2
$$ x\left(\frac{t}{2}\right) $$
- The key points move to:
  $$ 2T_1, \quad 2T_2 $$

So time scaling changes the **time locations**, but not the signal amplitudes.

---

### 7. Physical Interpretation of Time Scaling
A recorded signal can be played at different speeds.

- Playing it faster corresponds to **time compression**
- Playing it slower corresponds to **time expansion**

This is different from shifting:
- **shifting** changes when the signal starts
- **scaling** changes how quickly it runs

---

### 8. Example of Time Scaling
Given:
$$
x(t)=
\begin{cases}
2, & -1.5 \le t < 0 \\
2e^{-t/2}, & 0 \le t < 3 \\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

This signal is:
- constant at value $2$ from $t=-1.5$ to $t=0$
- then exponentially decaying from $t=0$ to $t=3$

---

### 9. Compressed Version of the Signal
Compress by a factor of $3$:
$$
x_c(t)=x(3t)
$$

Evaluate the time ranges:

- From
  $$ -1.5 \le 3t < 0 $$
  we get
  $$ -0.5 \le t < 0 $$

- From
  $$ 0 \le 3t < 3 $$
  we get
  $$ 0 \le t < 1 $$

So:
$$
x_c(t)=
\begin{cases}
2, & -0.5 \le t < 0 \\
2e^{-3t/2}, & 0 \le t < 1 \\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

This is the same signal, but it runs **3 times faster**.

---

### 10. Expanded Version of the Signal
Expand by a factor of $2$:
$$
x_e(t)=x\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)
$$

Evaluate the time ranges:

- From
  $$ -1.5 \le \frac{t}{2} < 0 $$
  we get
  $$ -3 \le t < 0 $$

- From
  $$ 0 \le \frac{t}{2} < 3 $$
  we get
  $$ 0 \le t < 6 $$

So:
$$
x_e(t)=
\begin{cases}
2, & -3 \le t < 0 \\
2e^{-t/4}, & 0 \le t < 6 \\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

This is the same signal, but it runs **2 times slower**.

---

### 11. Time Reversal
Time reversal flips a signal across the vertical axis $t=0$.

The operation is:
$$ x(-t) $$

Interpretation:
- whatever was at time $t=a$ moves to time $t=-a$
- the signal is mirrored in time

A physical analogy is playing a recording **backward**.

---

### 12. General Interpretation of Time Reversal
If a signal originally rises, peaks, and then falls as time moves forward, then $x(-t)$ shows that same behavior in reverse order.

So time reversal changes the **direction of time** in the signal.

---

### 13. Example of Time Reversal
Given:
$$
x(t)=
\begin{cases}
e^{t/2}, & -5 < t \le -1 \\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

This signal exists only between $t=-5$ and $t=-1$.

Apply time reversal:
$$
x(-t)=e^{-t/2}
$$

Now transform the time range:
$$
-5 < -t \le -1
$$

Multiply through by $-1$ and reverse the inequalities:
$$
1 \le t < 5
$$

So the reversed signal is:
$$
x(-t)=
\begin{cases}
e^{-t/2}, & 1 \le t < 5 \\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

---

### 14. Effect of Time Reversal in the Example
- Original signal existed on:
  $$ -5 < t \le -1 $$
- Reversed signal exists on:
  $$ 1 \le t < 5 $$

So:
- the support flips from negative time to positive time
- an increasing exponential becomes a decreasing exponential after reversal

---

### 15. Main Takeaways
- **Time shift**:
  - delay:
    $$ x(t-T) $$
  - advance:
    $$ x(t+T) $$

- **Time scaling**:
  - compression:
    $$ x(at), \quad a>1 $$
  - expansion:
    $$ x\left(\frac{t}{a}\right), \quad a>1 $$

- **Time reversal**:
  $$ x(-t) $$

These three operations are basic tools for analyzing and manipulating signals in time-domain signal processing.
