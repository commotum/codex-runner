## Lecture Outline (Course Review and Wrap-Up)

### 1. Fundamentals of Discrete-Time Signals and Systems
- Discrete-time signals and their transformations
- Linear and time-invariant systems
- Causal systems
- BIBO stable systems
- Decomposition of signals into scaled and shifted impulses:
  $$ x[n] = \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} x[m]\delta[n-m] $$

---

### 2. Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) Systems
- Unit impulse response:
  $$ h[n] = \mathcal{H}\{\delta[n]\} $$
- Every LTI system performs convolution
- Testing systems for causality and stability
- Relationship between input $x[n]$, impulse response $h[n]$, and output $y[n]$

---

### 3. Convolution
- Convolution as the input-output rule for LTI systems:
  $$ y[n] = \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} x[m]\,h[n-m] $$
- Convolution as a sliding-window operation
- FIR filtering as weighted averaging
- Software and hardware views of convolution

---

### 4. FIR Filter Implementation
- Implementing FIR filters with:
  - delay elements
  - multipliers
  - adders
- Tapped-delay-line interpretation
- FIR systems as finite-length convolution:
  $$ y[n] = \sum_{m=0}^{N-1} h[m]\,x[n-m] $$

---

### 5. Frequency Response of LTI Systems
- Response of an LTI system to a complex sinusoid:
  $$ x[n] = e^{j\omega n} \quad \Rightarrow \quad y[n] = H(e^{j\omega})e^{j\omega n} $$
- Frequency response depends on:
  - the frequency $\omega$
  - the impulse response $h[n]$
- LTI systems are frequency selective
- Frequency response is the Fourier transform of the impulse response:
  $$ H(e^{j\omega}) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} h[n]e^{-j\omega n} $$

---

### 6. Combinations of LTI Systems
- Parallel combinations
- Series (cascade) combinations
- More complex interconnections of LTI systems
- Overall system behavior from combining simpler blocks

---

### 7. Basic Design of FIR Filters
- Truncation of ideal impulse responses
- Shifting filters to make them causal
- Transforming lowpass designs into:
  - highpass filters
  - bandpass filters
  - bandstop filters

---

### 8. Discrete-Time Fourier Transform (DTFT)
- DTFT as the same transform used for frequency response
- Forward DTFT:
  $$ X(e^{j\omega}) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} x[n]e^{-j\omega n} $$
- Inverse DTFT:
  $$ x[n] = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} X(e^{j\omega})e^{j\omega n}\,d\omega $$

---

### 9. DTFT Properties
- Linearity
- Periodicity
- Symmetry
- Delay (time shift)
- Convolution
- Modulation
- Multiplication

---

### 10. Fourier Series Expansion of Periodic Discrete-Time Signals
- Periodic signals expanded as sums of harmonically related complex exponentials
- Discrete Fourier series form:
  $$ x[n] = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} X[k]e^{j\frac{2\pi}{N}kn} $$
