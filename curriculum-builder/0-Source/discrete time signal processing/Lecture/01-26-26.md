## Lecture Outline (LTI Systems and Convolution)

### 1. Core Topics
- Time-invariant systems
- Linear & time-invariant (LTI) systems
- Convolution
- LTI systems are frequency-selective
- Frequency response (introduced as a coming topic)

---

### 2. Time Invariance
- System notation:
  $$ y[n] = \mathcal{H}\{x[n]\} $$
- Time invariance condition:
  $$ \mathcal{H}\{x[n-m]\} = y[n-m] $$

---

### 3. Example: Time-Invariant but Not Linear
- System:
  $$ y[n] = e^{x[n]} $$
- Time shift check:
  $$ \mathcal{H}\{x[n-m]\} = e^{x[n-m]} $$
  $$ y[n-m] = e^{x[n-m]} $$
  - Same $\Rightarrow$ time invariant
- Linearity fails (no additivity):
  $$ e^{x_1[n] + x_2[n]} \neq e^{x_1[n]} + e^{x_2[n]} $$

---

### 4. Example: Time-Varying System
- System:
  $$ y[n] = x[n] + n $$
- Time shift check:
  $$ \mathcal{H}\{x[n-m]\} = x[n-m] + n $$
  $$ y[n-m] = x[n-m] + (n-m) $$
- Not equal $\Rightarrow$ system is time varying

---

### 5. Every LTI System Performs Convolution
- Impulse decomposition of a signal:
  $$ x[n] = \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} x[m]\;\delta[n-m] $$
- Define impulse response:
  $$ h[n] = \mathcal{H}\{\delta[n]\} $$
- Apply system to the decomposition:
  $$ \mathcal{H}\left\{\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} x[m]\delta[n-m]\right\}
     = \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} x[m]\;\mathcal{H}\{\delta[n-m]\} $$
- By time invariance:
  $$ \mathcal{H}\{\delta[n-m]\} = h[n-m] $$
- Convolution result:
  $$ y[n] = \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} x[m]\;h[n-m] $$

---

### 6. Equivalent Convolution Form (Index Change)
- Let $k = n-m$ (so $m = n-k$), then:
  $$ y[n] = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} x[n-k]\;h[k] $$

---

### 7. Graphical / “Flip and Slide” Convolution Procedure
To compute $y[n]$ at a particular time $n$:
- Reflect one sequence (typically $x[k] \to x[-k]$)
- Shift by $n$ samples to form $x[n-k]$
- Multiply pointwise with $h[k]$:
  $$ x[n-k]\;h[k] $$
- Sum over all $k$:
  $$ y[n] = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} x[n-k]\;h[k] $$

---

### 8. Convolution with Finite-Length Impulse Responses
- If $h[n]$ is nonzero only over a finite interval (example shown: $h[n]=0$ outside a range),
  then many terms in the sum are zero:
  $$ h[n-m] = 0 \;\; \text{when } (n-m) \text{ is outside the support of } h[\cdot] $$
- This limits which $m$ (or $k$) actually contribute to $y[n]$
