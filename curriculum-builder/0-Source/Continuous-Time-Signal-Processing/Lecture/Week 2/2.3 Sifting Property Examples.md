## Lecture Outline (Examples of the Sampling / Sifting Property of the Unit Impulse)

### 1. Reminder: Sifting Property
- For a continuous function $f(t)$:
  $$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(t)\,\delta(t-T)\,dt = f(T) $$
- Interpretation:
  - multiplying by a shifted impulse $\delta(t-T)$ and integrating
  - extracts the value of the function at $t=T$

---

### 2. Meaning of the Property
- The impulse acts like a sampling device.
- If the impulse is located at $t=T$, then the integral “picks out”:
  $$ f(T) $$
- This is why the property is also called the **sampling property** or **sifting property**.

---

### 3. Example 1: Sampling a Sine Function
- Evaluate:
  $$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \sin(t)\,\delta\!\left(t-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\,dt $$
- Apply the sifting property directly:
  $$ \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) $$
- Result:
  $$ \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} $$

---

### 4. Interpretation of Example 1
- The impulse is shifted to:
  $$ t=\frac{\pi}{4} $$
- So the integral extracts the value of the sinusoid at that location.
- The continuous-time signal is sampled at:
  $$ t=\frac{\pi}{4} $$

---

### 5. Example 2: Impulse Argument Not in Standard Form
- Evaluate:
  $$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \sin(2\pi t)\,\delta(4t-1)\,dt $$
- This is not immediately in the form:
  $$ \delta(t-T) $$
- So a change of variables is used.

---

### 6. Change of Variables
- Let:
  $$ \tau = 4t $$
- Then:
  $$ t = \frac{\tau}{4}, \qquad dt = \frac{1}{4}\,d\tau $$
- Substitute into the integral:
  $$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \sin(2\pi t)\,\delta(4t-1)\,dt
     =
     \frac{1}{4}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
     \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\tau\right)\delta(\tau-1)\,d\tau $$

---

### 7. Apply the Sifting Property After Substitution
- Now the impulse is in standard form:
  $$ \delta(\tau-1) $$
- So:
  $$ \frac{1}{4}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
     \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\tau\right)\delta(\tau-1)\,d\tau
     =
     \frac{1}{4}\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) $$
- Since:
  $$ \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=1 $$
- The result is:
  $$ \frac{1}{4} $$

---

### 8. Interpretation of Example 2
- Even when the impulse is written as:
  $$ \delta(4t-1) $$
  it still acts as a sampler after rewriting it into a usable form.
- The change of variables makes the sifting property easier to apply.

---

### 9. Main Takeaways
- The sifting property is:
  $$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(t)\,\delta(t-T)\,dt = f(T) $$
- Example 1:
  $$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \sin(t)\,\delta\!\left(t-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\,dt
     = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} $$
- Example 2:
  $$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \sin(2\pi t)\,\delta(4t-1)\,dt
     = \frac{1}{4} $$
- A shifted impulse lets you sample a continuous-time signal at a specific location.
