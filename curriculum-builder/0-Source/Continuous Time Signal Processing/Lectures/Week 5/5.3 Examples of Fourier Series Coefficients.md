## Lecture Outline (Complex Fourier Series Coefficients for a Periodic Exponential Signal)

### 1. Goal of the Lecture
- Continue Fourier series analysis by computing the **complex Fourier series coefficients**.
- Unlike the previous example, this signal produces coefficients that are generally **complex-valued**.

---

### 2. Signal Definition
- The signal is periodic with period:
  $$ T = \pi $$
- Over one period:
  $$ x(t) = e^{-t/2}, \quad 0 \le t < \pi $$
- Periodic extension:
  $$ x(t+\pi) = x(t) $$

---

### 3. Fundamental Frequency
- The fundamental frequency is:
  $$ \omega_0 = \frac{2\pi}{T} = \frac{2\pi}{\pi} = 2 $$

---

### 4. Complex Fourier Series Form
- A periodic signal can be written as:
  $$ x(t) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} a_k e^{jk\omega_0 t} $$
- For this signal:
  $$ x(t) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} a_k e^{jk(2)t} $$

---

### 5. Formula for the Fourier Coefficients
- The complex Fourier series coefficient is:
  $$ a_k = \frac{1}{T}\int_{\text{one period}} x(t)e^{-jk\omega_0 t}\,dt $$
- Since $T=\pi$ and $\omega_0=2$:
  $$ a_k = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi e^{-t/2}e^{-jk(2)t}\,dt $$

---

### 6. Evaluate the Integral
- Combine the exponentials:
  $$ a_k = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi e^{-(\frac12 + j2k)t}\,dt $$
- Integrating:
  $$ a_k = \frac{1}{\pi}\left[\frac{e^{-(\frac12+j2k)t}}{-(\frac12+j2k)}\right]_0^\pi $$
- Simplify:
  $$ a_k = \frac{1}{\pi(\frac12 + j2k)}\left(1 - e^{-(\frac12+j2k)\pi}\right) $$
- Since:
  $$ e^{-j2k\pi} = 1 $$
  we get:
  $$ a_k = \frac{1 - e^{-\pi/2}}{\pi(\frac12 + j2k)} $$
- Multiply numerator and denominator by $2$:
  $$ a_k = \frac{2(1-e^{-\pi/2})}{\pi(1+j4k)} $$
- Numerical form:
  $$ a_k \approx \frac{0.504}{1+j4k} $$

---

### 7. DC Component
- For $k=0$:
  $$ a_0 = \frac{2(1-e^{-\pi/2})}{\pi} \approx 0.504 $$
- This is the average value / DC component of the signal.

---

### 8. First Few Fourier Coefficients
- For $k=1$:
  $$ a_1 = \frac{0.504}{1+j4} = 0.122\,e^{-j75.96^\circ} $$
- For $k=-1$:
  $$ a_{-1} = \frac{0.504}{1-j4} = 0.122\,e^{j75.96^\circ} $$

- For $k=2$:
  $$ a_2 = \frac{0.504}{1+j8} = 0.0625\,e^{-j82.87^\circ} $$
- For $k=-2$:
  $$ a_{-2} = \frac{0.504}{1-j8} = 0.0625\,e^{j82.87^\circ} $$

---

### 9. Magnitude Spectrum
- The magnitude of the coefficients is:
  $$ |a_k| = \frac{0.504}{\sqrt{1+16k^2}} $$
- First few values:
  $$ |a_0| = 0.504 $$
  $$ |a_{\pm1}| = 0.122 $$
  $$ |a_{\pm2}| = 0.0625 $$

---

### 10. Phase Spectrum
- Since the numerator is real and positive:
  $$ \angle a_k = -\tan^{-1}(4k) $$
- First few phase values:
  $$ \angle a_0 = 0^\circ $$
  $$ \angle a_1 = -75.96^\circ $$
  $$ \angle a_{-1} = 75.96^\circ $$
  $$ \angle a_2 = -82.87^\circ $$
  $$ \angle a_{-2} = 82.87^\circ $$

---

### 11. Conjugate Symmetry in This Example
- The coefficients for positive and negative harmonics appear as complex conjugates:
  $$ a_{-k} = a_k^* $$
- This is visible in the example values:
  - same magnitude
  - opposite phase angles

---

### 12. Interpretation
- The signal can be reconstructed by adding infinitely many complex exponentials:
  $$ x(t) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} a_k e^{jk\omega_0 t} $$
- Each coefficient $a_k$ tells:
  - how much of the $k$th harmonic is present
  - the amplitude contribution through $|a_k|$
  - the phase contribution through $\angle a_k$

---

### 13. Main Takeaways
- For this periodic exponential signal:
  $$ T=\pi, \quad \omega_0=2 $$
- The complex Fourier coefficients are:
  $$ a_k = \frac{2(1-e^{-\pi/2})}{\pi(1+j4k)} \approx \frac{0.504}{1+j4k} $$
- Unlike the previous example, these coefficients are generally **complex**.
- To represent them, we use:
  - a **magnitude plot** of $|a_k|$ versus $k$
  - a **phase plot** of $\angle a_k$ versus $k$
