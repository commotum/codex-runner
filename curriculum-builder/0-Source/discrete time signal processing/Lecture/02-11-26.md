## Lecture Outline (BIBO Stability and Cascade of LTI Systems)

### 1. BIBO Stability Condition for LTI Systems
- For an LTI system:
  $$ y[n] = \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} h[m]\,x[n-m] $$
- BIBO stability ⇔ impulse response is absolutely summable:
  $$ \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} |h[n]| < \infty $$

---

### 2. Proof: Absolute Summability ⇒ BIBO Stability
- Assume bounded input:
  $$ |x[n]| \le M_x < \infty $$
- Then:
  $$
  |y[n]|
  = \left| \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} h[m]x[n-m] \right|
  \le \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} |h[m]|\,|x[n-m]|
  $$
- Using boundedness:
  $$
  |y[n]| \le M_x \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} |h[m]|
  $$
- If:
  $$ \sum |h[m]| = M_h < \infty $$
- Then:
  $$ |y[n]| \le M_x M_h < \infty $$
- Therefore: system is BIBO stable.

---

### 3. Converse: If Not Absolutely Summable ⇒ Not BIBO Stable
- Suppose:
  $$ \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} |h[n]| = \infty $$
- Choose input:
  $$ x[-m] = \text{sgn}(h[m]) $$
- Then:
  $$ h[m]x[-m] = |h[m]| $$
- So:
  $$ y[0] = \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} |h[m]| = \infty $$
- Output unbounded ⇒ system is not BIBO stable.

---

### 4. Stability Examples
#### (a) Oscillatory Impulse Response
- $$ h[n] = \cos(\omega_0 n)u[n] $$
- Not absolutely summable ⇒ not stable.

#### (b) Exponential Decay
- $$ h[n] = \alpha^n u[n] $$
- If:
  $$ |\alpha| < 1 $$
- Then:
  $$ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} |\alpha|^n = \frac{1}{1-|\alpha|} < \infty $$
- Stable.

#### (c) Harmonic Series
- $$ h[n] = \frac{1}{n+1} u[n] $$
- Since:
  $$ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n+1} = \infty $$
- Not stable.

---

### 5. FIR Filters
- Any finite-length impulse response:
  $$ h[n] = 0 \text{ outside a finite interval} $$
- Always absolutely summable.
- Therefore:
  > All FIR filters are BIBO stable.

---

### 6. Cascade (Series) Combination of LTI Systems
- Two systems:
  $$ x[n] \xrightarrow{h_1[n]} v[n] \xrightarrow{h_2[n]} y[n] $$
- Intermediate output:
  $$ v[n] = x[n] * h_1[n] $$
- Final output:
  $$ y[n] = v[n] * h_2[n] $$

---

### 7. Overall Impulse Response of Cascade
- Input: $$ \delta[n] $$
- Output:
  $$ h[n] = h_1[n] * h_2[n] $$
- Convolution form:
  $$ h[n] = \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} h_1[m]\,h_2[n-m] $$

---

### 8. Frequency Response of Cascaded Systems
- Individual responses:
  $$ H_1(e^{j\omega}), \quad H_2(e^{j\omega}) $$
- Overall frequency response:
  $$ H(e^{j\omega}) = H_1(e^{j\omega})H_2(e^{j\omega}) $$

---

### 9. Proof of Multiplication Property
- Start from:
  $$ H(e^{j\omega}) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} h[n]e^{-j\omega n} $$
- Substitute convolution:
  $$ h[n] = \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} h_1[m]h_2[n-m] $$
- Rearranging sums yields:
  $$
  H(e^{j\omega})
  = \left(\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} h_1[m]e^{-j\omega m}\right)
    \left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} h_2[k]e^{-j\omega k}\right)
  $$
- Therefore:
  $$ H(e^{j\omega}) = H_1(e^{j\omega})H_2(e^{j\omega}) $$
