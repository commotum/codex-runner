## Lecture Outline (Fourier Series Properties: Time Shifting)

### 1. Review of the Complex Fourier Series
- A periodic signal $x(t)$ can be represented in complex exponential form as:
  $$
  x(t) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} a_k e^{jk\omega_0 t}
  $$
- The Fourier series coefficients are given by:
  $$
  a_k = \frac{1}{T}\int_{\text{one period}} x(t)e^{-jk\omega_0 t}\,dt
  $$
- Here:
  - $T$ is the period
  - $\omega_0 = \frac{2\pi}{T}$ is the fundamental frequency
  - $a_k$ are the complex Fourier coefficients

---

### 2. Goal of This Lecture
- Study the **time-shifting property** of Fourier series.
- Main question:
  - If $x(t)$ has coefficients $a_k$, what happens to the coefficients if the signal is shifted in time to become:
    $$
    x(t-t_0)
    $$

---

### 3. Fourier Coefficients of a Shifted Signal
- Let the shifted signal be:
  $$
  x(t-t_0)
  $$
- Its Fourier coefficients are:
  $$
  a_k^{\text{shift}} = \frac{1}{T}\int_{\text{one period}} x(t-t_0)e^{-jk\omega_0 t}\,dt
  $$

---

### 4. Derivation Using a Change of Variables
- Let:
  $$
  \tau = t-t_0
  $$
  so that:
  $$
  t = \tau + t_0, \qquad dt = d\tau
  $$
- Substitute into the coefficient formula:
  $$
  a_k^{\text{shift}}
  =
  \frac{1}{T}\int x(\tau)e^{-jk\omega_0(\tau+t_0)}\,d\tau
  $$
- Split the exponential:
  $$
  a_k^{\text{shift}}
  =
  e^{-jk\omega_0 t_0}
  \frac{1}{T}\int x(\tau)e^{-jk\omega_0\tau}\,d\tau
  $$
- Recognize the remaining integral as the original coefficient $a_k$:
  $$
  a_k^{\text{shift}} = e^{-jk\omega_0 t_0}a_k
  $$

---

### 5. Time-Shifting Property
- Final result:
  $$
  x(t-t_0)
  \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad
  a_k^{\text{shift}} = e^{-jk\omega_0 t_0}a_k
  $$
- This is the Fourier series **time-shifting property**.

---

### 6. Effect on Magnitude and Phase
- Since:
  $$
  \left|e^{-jk\omega_0 t_0}\right| = 1
  $$
  the magnitude does not change:
  $$
  \left|a_k^{\text{shift}}\right| = |a_k|
  $$
- The phase changes by:
  $$
  \angle a_k^{\text{shift}} = \angle a_k - k\omega_0 t_0
  $$
- So:
  - **time shifting does not change the magnitude spectrum**
  - **time shifting adds a linear phase shift**

---

### 7. Example Signal from a Previous Problem
- Consider a periodic rectangular signal $x(t)$ with:
  - period:
    $$ T = 4 $$
  - fundamental frequency:
    $$ \omega_0 = \frac{2\pi}{T} = \frac{\pi}{2} $$
- From the earlier problem, its Fourier coefficients are:
  $$
  a_0 = \frac{1}{2}
  $$
  $$
  a_k = \frac{\sin\left(k\pi/2\right)}{k\pi}, \qquad k \ne 0
  $$

---

### 8. New Signal to Analyze
- The lecture defines a new signal:
  $$
  g(t) = x(t-1) - \frac{1}{2}
  $$
- This includes:
  1. a time shift by $1$ second
  2. a downward vertical shift by $\frac{1}{2}$

---

### 9. Fourier Coefficients of the Shifted Part
- For the shifted part $x(t-1)$:
  $$
  c_k = e^{-jk\omega_0(1)}a_k
  $$
- Since $\omega_0 = \frac{\pi}{2}$:
  $$
  c_k = e^{-jk\pi/2}a_k
  $$

---

### 10. Fourier Coefficients of the Constant Shift
- The constant term:
  $$
  -\frac{1}{2}
  $$
  contributes only to the DC coefficient.
- Its Fourier coefficients are:
  $$
  d_k =
  \begin{cases}
  -\frac{1}{2}, & k=0 \\
  0, & k\ne 0
  \end{cases}
  $$

---

### 11. Final Fourier Coefficients of $g(t)$
- Combine the two parts:
  $$
  G_k = c_k + d_k
  $$
- Therefore:
  $$
  G_k =
  \begin{cases}
  a_0 - \frac{1}{2}, & k=0 \\
  e^{-jk\pi/2}a_k, & k\ne 0
  \end{cases}
  $$
- Since $a_0=\frac{1}{2}$:
  $$
  G_0 = 0
  $$
- And for $k\ne 0$:
  $$
  G_k = e^{-jk\pi/2}\frac{\sin(k\pi/2)}{k\pi}
  $$

---

### 12. Why This Property Is Useful
- Without the time-shifting property, the new coefficients would need to be recomputed from scratch.
- With the property, we can:
  - reuse the original coefficients
  - multiply by a simple phase factor
  - adjust the DC term separately
- This makes Fourier series problems much faster and easier.

---

### 13. Main Takeaways
- If:
  $$
  x(t) \Longleftrightarrow a_k
  $$
  then:
  $$
  x(t-t_0) \Longleftrightarrow e^{-jk\omega_0 t_0}a_k
  $$
- Time shifting:
  - leaves the magnitude spectrum unchanged
  - changes only the phase
- In the example:
  $$
  g(t)=x(t-1)-\frac{1}{2}
  $$
  has coefficients:
  $$
  G_0=0
  $$
  $$
  G_k = e^{-jk\pi/2}\frac{\sin(k\pi/2)}{k\pi}, \qquad k\ne 0
  $$
