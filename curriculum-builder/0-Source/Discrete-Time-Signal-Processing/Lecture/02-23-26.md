## Lecture Outline (Highpass / Bandpass via Ideal Filters + Convolution Support/Overlap)

### 1. Ideal Highpass Filter as Complement of Lowpass
- Frequency response idea (sketch):
  - Passes high frequencies, rejects low frequencies around $\omega=0$
- Relationship:
  $$ H_{HP}(e^{j\omega}) = 1 - H_{LP}(e^{j\omega}) $$

---

### 2. Direct Inverse DTFT Derivation of Highpass Impulse Response
- Inverse DTFT:
  $$ h[n] = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} H(e^{j\omega})e^{j\omega n}\,d\omega $$
- For ideal highpass (integrate over the passbands):
  $$ h[n] = \frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\int_{-\pi}^{-\omega_c} e^{j\omega n}\,d\omega
  + \int_{\omega_c}^{\pi} e^{j\omega n}\,d\omega\right) $$
- Result (piecewise):
  $$
  h_{HP}[n] =
  \begin{cases}
    \dfrac{\pi-\omega_c}{\pi}, & n=0 \\
    -\dfrac{\sin(\omega_c n)}{\pi n}, & n\ne 0
  \end{cases}
  $$

---

### 3. “Difference of Two Frequency Responses” View
- All-pass (unity) filter:
  $$ H_{\text{allpass}}(e^{j\omega}) = 1 $$
  $$ h_{\text{allpass}}[n] = \delta[n] $$
- Lowpass impulse response (recall):
  $$
  h_{LP}[n] =
  \begin{cases}
    \dfrac{\omega_c}{\pi}, & n=0 \\
    \dfrac{\sin(\omega_c n)}{\pi n}, & n\ne 0
  \end{cases}
  $$
- Therefore:
  $$ h_{HP}[n] = h_{\text{allpass}}[n] - h_{LP}[n] = \delta[n] - h_{LP}[n] $$

---

### 4. Highpass via Modulation (Frequency Shift by $\pi$)
- “Use modulation property” to shift a lowpass prototype:
  $$ h_{HP}[n] = h_{LP}[n]e^{j\pi n} $$
- Using:
  $$ e^{j\pi n} = \cos(\pi n) + j\sin(\pi n) $$
  and for integer $n$, $\sin(\pi n)=0$ so:
  $$ e^{j\pi n} = \cos(\pi n) = (-1)^n $$
- Trig identity work shows it matches:
  $$ h_{HP}[n] =
  \begin{cases}
    1-\dfrac{\omega_c}{\pi}, & n=0 \\
    -\dfrac{\sin(\omega_c n)}{\pi n}, & n\ne 0
  \end{cases}
  $$

---

### 5. Bandpass Filter Construction (Difference of Two Lowpasses)
- Sketch shows a bandpass made by subtracting two rectangular lowpass responses:
  - Wide lowpass minus narrow lowpass → leaves a “ring” (band) in frequency
- Typical form:
  $$ H_{BP}(e^{j\omega}) = H_{LP,\omega_u}(e^{j\omega}) - H_{LP,\omega_l}(e^{j\omega}) $$
- Corresponding impulse response:
  $$ h_{BP}[n] = h_{LP,\omega_u}[n] - h_{LP,\omega_l}[n] $$

---

### 6. Convolution: Support, Overlap, and When Output Is Nonzero
- Setup uses finite-support sequences:
  - $x[m]$ supported on $m\in[N_1,\,N_2]$
  - $h[m]$ supported on $m\in[M_1,\,M_2]$
- Convolution:
  $$ y[n] = \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} x[m]\,h[n-m] $$

---

### 7. First and Last Time Overlap Occurs (Start/End of Nonzero Output)
- “First overlap” condition (convolution starts):
  $$ -M_1 + n = N_1 \;\Rightarrow\; n = N_1 + M_1 $$
- “Last overlap” condition (last nonzero sample):
  $$ -M_2 + n = N_2 \;\Rightarrow\; n = N_2 + M_2 $$
- Therefore:
  $$ y[n]\ne 0 \text{ only for } N_1+M_1 \le n \le N_2+M_2 $$

---

### 8. Partial vs Full Overlap Ranges (Summation Limits)
- Partial overlap (going in) occurs when the shifted $h[n-m]$ just begins to overlap $x[m]$
- Full overlap occurs when the entire support of one lies inside the other:
  $$ -M_2+n \ge N_1 \quad \text{and} \quad -M_1+n \le N_2 $$
  which gives:
  $$ n \ge N_1+M_2 \quad \text{and} \quad n \le N_2+M_1 $$
- In overlap, $m$ runs over the intersection:
  $$ -M_2+n \le m \le -M_1+n $$
- Partial overlap (going out) similarly adjusts the upper/lower limit, ending with:
  $$ \sum_{m=n-M_2}^{N_2} (\cdots) $$
