## Lecture Outline (Laplace Transform for LTI Systems: Transfer Function, Poles, and Stability)

### 1. From Signal Analysis to System Analysis
- Previously, Laplace transform was used from the **signal** perspective:
  $$ X(s) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-st}\,dt $$
- Here:
  $$ s = \sigma + j\omega $$
- Now the focus shifts to the **system** perspective, specifically for **LTI systems**.

---

### 2. Impulse Response and Convolution
- For an LTI system:
  - input: $x(t)$
  - output: $y(t)$
  - impulse response: $h(t)$
- The output is given by convolution:
  $$ y(t) = x(t) * h(t) $$

---

### 3. Convolution in the Laplace Domain
- Taking the Laplace transform of convolution gives:
  $$ Y(s) = X(s)H(s) $$
- Therefore:
  $$ H(s) = \frac{Y(s)}{X(s)} $$
- This function $H(s)$ is called the:
  - **transfer function**
  - or **system function**

---

### 4. Rational Transfer Function Form
- In general, the transfer function is written as:
  $$ H(s) = \frac{N(s)}{D(s)} $$
- where:
  - $N(s)$ is the numerator polynomial
  - $D(s)$ is the denominator polynomial

---

### 5. Poles and Zeros
- **Zeros** are values of $s$ that make:
  $$ H(s) = 0 $$
  which come from:
  $$ N(s)=0 $$
- **Poles** are values of $s$ that make:
  $$ H(s)\to\infty $$
  which come from:
  $$ D(s)=0 $$
- Example form:
  $$ H(s)=\frac{s-b}{(s-a_1)(s-a_2)} $$
- Then:
  - zero at:
    $$ s=b $$
  - poles at:
    $$ s=a_1,\; a_2 $$

---

### 6. The $s$-Plane
- The $s$-plane has:
  - horizontal axis: $\sigma$
  - vertical axis: $j\omega$
- Pole locations can be:
  - real
  - complex
  - positive or negative in real part

---

### 7. Causal Systems
- A system is **causal** if:
  $$ h(t)=0 \quad \text{for } t<0 $$
- Practical systems are usually causal.
- Stability discussion in this lecture is focused on **causal LTI systems**.

---

### 8. Main Stability Result
- For a **causal system with rational $H(s)$**:
  - the system is **BIBO stable** if and only if **all poles lie in the left half-plane**
- That means:
  $$ \Re\{s\}<0 $$
  for every pole

---

### 9. Why Poles Matter for Stability
- Stability is determined by the poles of the transfer function.
- The zeros are not the main concern for BIBO stability here.
- If poles lie in the left half-plane, the system response decays over time.
- If any pole lies in the right half-plane, the response grows and the system is unstable.

---

### 10. Example: Series RLC Circuit
- Consider a series circuit with:
  - resistor $R$
  - inductor $L$
  - capacitor $C$
- Input:
  $$ x(t)=v_s(t) $$
- Output:
  $$ y(t)=v_C(t) $$
  where $v_C(t)$ is the capacitor voltage

---

### 11. Differential Equation for the RLC System
- Apply KVL:
  $$ v_s(t)=i(t)R + L\frac{di(t)}{dt} + v_C(t) $$
- For the capacitor:
  $$ i(t)=C\frac{dv_C(t)}{dt} $$
- Substitute $v_C(t)=y(t)$ and $v_s(t)=x(t)$:
  $$ x(t)=y(t)+RC\frac{dy(t)}{dt}+LC\frac{d^2y(t)}{dt^2} $$

---

### 12. Convert the Differential Equation to the Laplace Domain
- Assuming zero initial conditions:
  $$ X(s)=Y(s)+RC\,sY(s)+LC\,s^2Y(s) $$
- Rearranging:
  $$ X(s)=Y(s)\left(1+RC\,s+LC\,s^2\right) $$

---

### 13. Transfer Function of the RLC Circuit
- Divide by $X(s)$:
  $$ H(s)=\frac{Y(s)}{X(s)}=\frac{1}{LC\,s^2+RC\,s+1} $$
- Equivalent normalized form:
  $$ H(s)=\frac{1/LC}{s^2+\frac{R}{L}s+\frac{1}{LC}} $$

---

### 14. Numerical Example
- Given:
  $$ R=10\;\omega,\quad L=1\;\text{H},\quad C=0.01\;\text{F} $$
- Then:
  $$ \frac{R}{L}=10,\qquad \frac{1}{LC}=100 $$
- So the denominator becomes:
  $$ s^2+10s+100 $$

---

### 15. Find the Poles
- Solve:
  $$ s^2+10s+100=0 $$
- The poles are:
  $$ s=-5 \pm j\,8.66 $$

---

### 16. Stability of the RLC Example
- Both poles satisfy:
  $$ \Re\{s\}=-5<0 $$
- So both poles lie in the **left half-plane**
- Therefore, the RLC system is:
  $$ \text{BIBO stable} $$

---

### 17. Pole Location and Time-Domain Behavior
- **Complex poles in the left half-plane**:
  - produce a **decaying oscillatory** impulse response
- **Real poles on the negative real axis**:
  - produce a **purely decaying exponential** response
- **Any pole in the right half-plane**:
  - produces a **growing exponential** response
  - makes the system unstable

---

### 18. Time-Domain Interpretation of Stable vs. Unstable Systems
- If poles are in the left half-plane:
  - the impulse response $h(t)$ decays
  - the output remains bounded for bounded input
- If a pole is in the right half-plane:
  - $h(t)$ grows with time
  - the output can blow up
  - the system is unstable

---

### 19. Main Takeaways
- For LTI systems:
  $$ Y(s)=X(s)H(s) $$
- The transfer function is:
  $$ H(s)=\frac{Y(s)}{X(s)} $$
- Stability is determined by the poles of $H(s)$
- For a **causal rational LTI system**:
  $$ \text{stable} \iff \text{all poles lie in the left half-plane} $$
- The RLC example illustrates how to:
  1. derive the differential equation
  2. convert it to the Laplace domain
  3. form the transfer function
  4. find poles
  5. determine stability
