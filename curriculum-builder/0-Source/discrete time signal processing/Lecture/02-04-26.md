## Lecture Outline (Convolution Practice, Frequency Response Zeros, and Causality)

### 1. Convolution of Two Finite-Length Step Windows
- Signals:
  $$ x[n] = u[n-5] - u[n-13] $$
  $$ h[n] = u[n] - u[n-5] $$
- Support / lengths:
  - $x[n]$ is nonzero for $5 \le n \le 12$ (8 samples)
  - $h[n]$ is nonzero for $0 \le n \le 4$ (5 samples)
- Convolution output nonzero range:
  $$ n_{\min} = N_1 + M_1 $$
  $$ n_{\max} = N_2 + M_2 $$
  (first overlap and last overlap conditions)
- Number of nonzero output samples:
  $$ (M_2-M_1) + (N_2-N_1) + 1 = (\text{len}(x) + \text{len}(h) - 1) $$
- For this example:
  $$ y[n] \neq 0 \text{ only for } 5 \le n \le 16 $$

---

### 2. Computing Convolution Output by Overlap Counting
- As the 5-sample window slides across the 8-sample window, overlap increases then decreases
- Output values (overlap count pattern):
  $$ y[5]=1,\; y[6]=2,\; y[7]=3,\; y[8]=4,\; y[9]=5 $$
  $$ y[10]=5,\; y[11]=5,\; y[12]=5 $$
  $$ y[13]=4,\; y[14]=3,\; y[15]=2,\; y[16]=1 $$
- Zero elsewhere:
  $$ y[n]=0 \text{ for } n<5 \text{ and } n>16 $$

---

### 3. Problem 2: Sinusoid Through a Moving-Sum FIR Filter
- Input:
  $$ x[n] = \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{4}n\right) $$
- FIR impulse response (length 8 moving-sum):
  $$
  h[n] =
  \begin{cases}
  1, & n=0,1,\dots,7 \\
  0, & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
  $$
- Convolution reduces to a finite sum:
  $$ y[n] = \sum_{m=n-7}^{n} x[m] = \sum_{m=n-7}^{n}\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{4}m\right) $$

---

### 4. Complex Exponential / Geometric-Series Evaluation
- Use:
  $$ \sin(\omega m) = \frac{1}{2j}\left(e^{j\omega m}-e^{-j\omega m}\right) $$
- Recognize finite geometric sums:
  $$ 1+\alpha+\alpha^2+\dots+\alpha^N = \frac{1-\alpha^{N+1}}{1-\alpha} $$
- Apply to the 8-term sum at $\omega=\frac{\pi}{4}$

---

### 5. Frequency Response and Zeros of a Rectangular FIR
- Frequency response:
  $$ H(e^{j\omega}) = \sum_{n=0}^{7} h[n]e^{-j\omega n} = \sum_{n=0}^{7} e^{-j\omega n} $$
- Closed form:
  $$ H(e^{j\omega}) = \frac{1-e^{-j\omega 8}}{1-e^{-j\omega}} $$
- At $\omega=\frac{\pi}{4}$:
  $$ e^{-j\omega 8} = e^{-j2\pi}=1 \;\Rightarrow\; H(e^{j\pi/4})=0 $$

---

### 6. Variant: Length-7 Moving-Sum Filter
- If:
  $$
  h[n] =
  \begin{cases}
  1, & n=0,1,\dots,6 \\
  0, & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
  $$
- Then:
  $$ H(e^{j\omega})=\sum_{n=0}^{6}e^{-j\omega n}=\frac{1-e^{-j\omega 7}}{1-e^{-j\omega}} $$
- Sinusoid output form (magnitude + phase shift):
  $$ y[n] = \left|H(e^{j\omega_0})\right|\sin\!\left(\omega_0 n + \theta(\omega_0)\right) $$

### 7. Problem 3: Ramp From Summed Steps
- Signal:
  $$ x[n] = (n+1)u[n] $$
- Relationship to step accumulation:
  $$ r[n] = \sum_{m=-\infty}^{n} u[m] $$
- Interpreting systems that compute:
  $$ y[n] = \sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} x[m]\,u[n-m] $$
  as a running sum / accumulator

---

### 8. Causality in LTI Systems
- Example impulse response (nonzero at negative indices implies noncausal behavior)
- Causality condition for LTI systems:
  $$ \text{LTI system is causal} \iff h[n]=0 \text{ for } n<0 $$
