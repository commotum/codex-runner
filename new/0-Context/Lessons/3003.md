## Introduction

Let's recall the multiplication law for conditional probability for two events $A$ and $B$:

$$
P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}
$$

Now suppose that $X$ and $Y$ are discrete random variables. The conditional probability that $X=x$ given that $Y=y$ follows immediately from the multiplication law and is given by

$$
P(X=x \mid Y=y) = \frac{P(X=x,\;Y=y)}{P(Y=y)} = \frac{f(x,y)}{f_Y(y)}
$$

where $f(x,y)$ is the joint PMF of $X$ and $Y$, and $f_Y(y)$ is the marginal mass function of $Y$.

To avoid getting a zero in the denominator, this formula assumes that

$$
P(Y=y)=f_Y(y)\ne 0
$$

If $P(Y=y)=0$, then the corresponding conditional probability for $X$ does not exist. It makes no sense to ask whether $X=x$ occurs *given that* $Y=y$ has occurred if the event $Y=y$ is impossible.

Similarly, the conditional probability that $Y=y$ given that $X=x$ is

$$
P(Y=y \mid X=x) = \frac{P(X=x,\;Y=y)}{P(X=x)} = \frac{f(x,y)}{f_X(x)}
$$

where $f_X(x)$ is the marginal mass function of $X$. This formula assumes that

$$
P(X=x)\ne 0
$$

## Computing a Conditional Probability

**Example:** Compute $P(Y=0 \mid X=1)$ given that the discrete random variables $X$ and $Y$ have the joint probability mass function $f(x,y)$ shown below.

| $f(x,y)$ | $Y=0$ | $Y=1$ |
|---|---:|---:|
| $X=0$ | $0.5$ | $0.05$ |
| $X=1$ | $0.15$ | $0.3$ |

**Explanation**

By the multiplication law for conditional probability,

$$
P(Y=0 \mid X=1) = \frac{P(X=1,\;Y=0)}{P(X=1)} = \frac{f(1,0)}{f_X(1)}
$$

Recall that the marginal distribution for $X$ corresponds to the row totals. Thus,

$$
f_X(1) = f(1,0) + f(1,1) = 0.15 + 0.3 = 0.45
$$

From the given table, we have

$$
f(1,0)=0.15
$$

Therefore,

$$
P(Y=0 \mid X=1) = \frac{0.15}{0.45} = \frac{1}{3}
$$

## Computing a Conditional Probability Over an Interval

**Example:** Compute $P(0<Y<3 \mid X=0)$ given that the discrete random variables $X$ and $Y$ have the joint probability mass function $f(x,y)$ shown below. Round your answer to two decimal places.

| $f(x,y)$ | $Y=0$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=2$ | $Y=3$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $X=0$ | $0.2$ | $0$ | $0.05$ | $0.1$ |
| $X=2$ | $0.1$ | $0.05$ | $0.15$ | $0$ |
| $X=4$ | $0.2$ | $0.05$ | $0$ | $0.1$ |

**Explanation**

By the multiplication law for conditional probability,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(0<Y<3 \mid X=0)
&= \frac{P(X=0,\;0<Y<3)}{P(X=0)} \\
&= \frac{P(X=0,\;Y=1)+P(X=0,\;Y=2)}{P(X=0)} \\
&= \frac{f(0,1)+f(0,2)}{f_X(0)}
\end{aligned}
$$

Recall that the marginal distribution for $X$ corresponds to the row totals. Thus,

$$
f_X(0) = f(0,0)+f(0,1)+f(0,2)+f(0,3) = 0.2+0+0.05+0.1 = 0.35
$$

From the given table, we have

$$
f(0,1)=0
\qquad \text{and} \qquad
f(0,2)=0.05
$$

Therefore,

$$
P(0<Y<3 \mid X=0) = \frac{0+0.05}{0.35} \approx 0.14
$$

## Conditional Probability Mass Functions

We can now define two more probability mass functions.

The **conditional probability mass function of $X$ given that $Y=y$**, denoted $f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid y)$, is defined as

$$
f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid y)
= P(X=x \mid Y=y)
= \frac{P(X=x,\;Y=y)}{P(Y=y)}
= \frac{f(x,y)}{f_Y(y)}
$$

where $f(x,y)$ is the joint probability mass function of $X$ and $Y$, and $f_Y(y)$ is the marginal mass function of $Y$.

The **conditional probability mass function of $Y$ given that $X=x$**, denoted $f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x)$, is defined as

$$
f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x)
= P(Y=y \mid X=x)
= \frac{P(X=x,\;Y=y)}{P(X=x)}
= \frac{f(x,y)}{f_X(x)}
$$

where $f(x,y)$ is the joint probability mass function of $X$ and $Y$, and $f_X(x)$ is the marginal mass function of $X$.

**Watch out:** in general,

$$
f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid y) \ne f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x)
$$

Finally, if $X$ and $Y$ are independent, then

$$
f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid y)=f_X(x),
\qquad
f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x)=f_Y(y)
$$

In other words, if $X$ and $Y$ are independent, then knowing the value of one random variable does not affect the probability distribution of the other.

## Computing the Value of a Conditional Probability Mass Function at a Point

**Example:** Compute $f_{X\mid Y}(2 \mid 2)$ given that the discrete random variables $X$ and $Y$ have the joint probability mass function $f(x,y)$ shown below.

| $f(x,y)$ | $Y=0$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=2$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $X=1$ | $\frac{1}{6}$ | $\frac{1}{9}$ | $\frac{1}{6}$ |
| $X=2$ | $\frac{1}{18}$ | $\frac{1}{18}$ | $\frac{1}{6}$ |
| $X=3$ | $\frac{1}{9}$ | $\frac{1}{9}$ | $\frac{1}{18}$ |

**Explanation**

The conditional probability mass function $f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid y)$ is defined as

$$
f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid y)=P(X=x \mid Y=y)
$$

Therefore,

$$
f_{X\mid Y}(2 \mid 2)=P(X=2 \mid Y=2)
$$

By the multiplication law for conditional probability,

$$
P(X=2 \mid Y=2)=\frac{P(X=2,\;Y=2)}{P(Y=2)}=\frac{f(2,2)}{f_Y(2)}
$$

Recall that the marginal distribution for $Y$ corresponds to the column totals. Thus,

$$
f_Y(2)=f(1,2)+f(2,2)+f(3,2)=\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{18}=\frac{7}{18}
$$

From the given table,

$$
f(2,2)=\frac{1}{6}
$$

Therefore,

$$
f_{X\mid Y}(2 \mid 2)=\frac{\frac{1}{6}}{\frac{7}{18}}=\frac{3}{7}
$$

## Computing a Conditional Probability Mass Function

**Example:** Find the conditional probability mass function $f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid 1)$ given that the random variables $X$ and $Y$ have the joint probability mass function $f(x,y)$ shown below.

| $f(x,y)$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=3$ | $Y=5$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $X=0$ | $0.1$ | $0$ | $0.2$ |
| $X=2$ | $0.3$ | $0.1$ | $0$ |
| $X=4$ | $0.1$ | $0$ | $0.2$ |

**Explanation**

The conditional probability mass function $f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid y)$ is defined as

$$
f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid y)=P(X=x \mid Y=y)
$$

So we need to compute

$$
f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid 1)=P(X=x \mid Y=1)
$$

By the multiplication law for conditional probability,

$$
P(X=x \mid Y=1)=\frac{P(X=x,\;Y=1)}{P(Y=1)}=\frac{f(x,1)}{f_Y(1)}
$$

Recall that the marginal distribution for $Y$ corresponds to the column totals. Thus,

$$
f_Y(1)=f(0,1)+f(2,1)+f(4,1)=0.1+0.3+0.1=0.5
$$

Now compute each value:

$$
P(X=0 \mid Y=1)=\frac{0.1}{0.5}=\frac{1}{5}
$$

$$
P(X=2 \mid Y=1)=\frac{0.3}{0.5}=\frac{3}{5}
$$

$$
P(X=4 \mid Y=1)=\frac{0.1}{0.5}=\frac{1}{5}
$$

Therefore, the conditional probability mass function is

| $x$ | $0$ | $2$ | $4$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid 1)$ | $\frac{1}{5}$ | $\frac{3}{5}$ | $\frac{1}{5}$ |
