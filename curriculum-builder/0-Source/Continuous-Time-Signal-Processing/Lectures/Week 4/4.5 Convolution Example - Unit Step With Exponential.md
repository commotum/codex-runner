## Lecture Outline (Convolution of a Unit Step with a Decaying Exponential)

### 1. Problem Setup
- The system has impulse response:
  $$
  h(t) = e^{-at}u(t)
  $$
  where $u(t)$ is the unit step function.
- The input is a step:
  $$
  x(t) = u(t)
  $$
- The goal is to compute the output:
  $$
  y(t) = h(t) * u(t)
  $$

---

### 2. Physical Motivation
- This models the response of a system with a decaying exponential impulse response to a sudden input change.
- The lecture notes that this is similar to the step response of many RC low-pass circuits.

---

### 3. Convolution Integral
- The output is found from:
  $$
  y(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(\tau)\,u(t-\tau)\,d\tau
  $$
- Important interpretation:
  - $t$ is the time at which the output is being evaluated
  - $\tau$ is the dummy variable that sweeps across the full time axis

---

### 4. Why $\tau$ Appears
- To compute $y(t)$ at one time instant, we do **not** just look at $h(t)$ and $u(t)$.
- Instead, we must examine the entire waveforms through:
  $$
  h(\tau) \quad \text{and} \quad u(t-\tau)
  $$
- This is why convolution uses an integral over all $\tau$.

---

### 5. Graphical Interpretation of the Signals
- The impulse response in integration form is:
  $$
  h(\tau) = e^{-a\tau}u(\tau)
  $$
- The shifted step becomes:
  $$
  u(t-\tau)
  $$
- This is obtained by:
  - flipping the step in $\tau$
  - shifting it to the right by $t$

---

### 6. Determine the Overlap Region for $t>0$
- For $\tau < 0$:
  $$
  h(\tau)=0
  $$
  so the product is zero.
- For $\tau > t$:
  $$
  u(t-\tau)=0
  $$
  so the product is zero.
- Therefore, the only nonzero interval is:
  $$
  0 \le \tau \le t
  $$
- On that interval:
  $$
  h(\tau)u(t-\tau)=e^{-a\tau}
  $$

---

### 7. Evaluate the Convolution Integral
- For $t>0$:
  $$
  y(t)=\int_0^t e^{-a\tau}\,d\tau
  $$
- Integrating:
  $$
  y(t)=\frac{1}{a}\left(1-e^{-at}\right)
  $$

---

### 8. Case for $t<0$
- If $t<0$, the shifted step does not overlap the nonzero part of $h(\tau)$.
- So:
  $$
  y(t)=0
  $$

---

### 9. Final Step Response
- Piecewise form:
  $$
  y(t)=
  \begin{cases}
  \frac{1}{a}\left(1-e^{-at}\right), & t \ge 0 \\
  0, & t < 0
  \end{cases}
  $$
- Compact form:
  $$
  y(t)=\frac{1}{a}\left(1-e^{-at}\right)u(t)
  $$

---

### 10. Shape of the Output
- The output is an **asymptotically increasing exponential**.
- It starts at:
  $$
  y(0)=0
  $$
- As $t \to \infty$:
  $$
  y(t)\to \frac{1}{a}
  $$

---

### 11. Reverse the Order of Convolution
- The lecture also computes:
  $$
  y(t)=u(t)*h(t)
  $$
- In integral form:
  $$
  y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} u(\tau)\,h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
  $$

---

### 12. Evaluate in the Reversed Order
- For $t>0$, the overlap is again:
  $$
  0 \le \tau \le t
  $$
- On that interval:
  $$
  u(\tau)h(t-\tau)=e^{-a(t-\tau)}
  $$
- So:
  $$
  y(t)=\int_0^t e^{-a(t-\tau)}\,d\tau
  $$
- This becomes:
  $$
  y(t)=e^{-at}\int_0^t e^{a\tau}\,d\tau
  $$
- Final result:
  $$
  y(t)=\frac{1}{a}\left(1-e^{-at}\right)
  $$

---

### 13. Key Conclusion
- Both orders give the same answer:
  $$
  h(t)*u(t)=u(t)*h(t)
  $$
- This demonstrates the **commutative property of convolution**.

---

### 14. Main Takeaways
- The step response of a decaying exponential impulse response is:
  $$
  y(t)=\frac{1}{a}\left(1-e^{-at}\right)u(t)
  $$
- Convolution is computed by:
  - flipping and shifting one signal
  - finding the overlap region
  - multiplying
  - integrating
- The variable $\tau$ is used because convolution depends on the entire histories of the signals, not just one instant.
