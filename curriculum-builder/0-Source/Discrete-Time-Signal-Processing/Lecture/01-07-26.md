## Lecture Outline

### 1. Signals and Information

- Definition of a signal as a function of one or more independent variables
  - Continuous-time signal: $x(t)$
  - Discrete-time signal: $x[n]$
  - Multivariable signals: $v(x,y,t)$
- Example of a real-world signal (e.g., gas level vs. distance traveled)
- Statement: *Signals contain information*

---

### 2. Continuous-Time vs. Discrete-Time Signals

- Continuous-time signals:
  - Independent variable varies over a continuous range
- Discrete-time signals:
  - Independent variable takes discrete values, typically integers
- Assumption of uniform sampling
- Relationship between continuous-time and discrete-time representations

---

### 3. Quantization and Digital Signals

- Quantization as discretization of amplitude
- Bit representation: each bit takes values $\{0,1\}$
- Digital signals:
  - Discrete in time and discrete in amplitude
- Analog signals:
  - Continuous in time and continuous in amplitude

---

### 4. Analog-to-Digital and Digital-to-Analog Systems

- System block diagram:
  $$ x(t) \rightarrow \text{A/D} \rightarrow x[n] \rightarrow \text{Digital Signal Processing} \rightarrow y[n] \rightarrow \text{D/A} \rightarrow y(t) $$
- Conceptual role of each block in signal processing systems

---

### 5. Sinusoidal Signals

- Definition of a cosine signal:
  $$ x(t) = \cos(\omega_0 t) $$
- Relationship between angular frequency and frequency:
  $$ \omega_0 = 2\pi f_0 $$
- Units:
  - $\omega_0$ in radians/second
  - $f_0$ in cycles/second (Hz)

---

### 6. Periodic Signals

- Definition of periodicity:
  $$ x(t) = x(t + T_0) $$
- Period–frequency relationship:
  $$ T_0 = \frac{1}{f_0} = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_0} $$
- Graphical examples of time shifting:
  $$ y(t) = x(t + T_0), \quad y(t) = x(t - 3) $$

---

### 7. Trigonometric Identities and Modulation

- Product-to-sum identity:
  $$ \cos A \cos B = \frac{1}{2}\left[\cos(A+B) + \cos(A-B)\right] $$
- Example:
  $$ 2\cos(\omega_0 t)\cos(\omega_1 t) = \cos((\omega_0+\omega_1)t) + \cos((\omega_0-\omega_1)t) $$
- Interpretation in the frequency domain (sum and difference frequencies)

---

### 8. Squaring a Cosine Signal

- Identity:
  $$ \cos^2 A = \frac{1}{2}\left(1 + \cos(2A)\right) $$
- Application to signal multiplication and frequency doubling

---

### 9. Complex Numbers and Geometry

- Representation of a complex number:
  $$ x = x_R + jx_I $$
- Magnitude:
  $$ r = \sqrt{x_R^2 + x_I^2} $$
- Trigonometric relationships:
  $$ x_R = r\cos\theta, \quad x_I = r\sin\theta $$
- Geometric interpretation in the complex plane (real and imaginary axes)
