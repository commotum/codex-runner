## Lecture Outline (Inverse DTFT and Ideal Filter Design)

### 1. Topics Being Connected
- Linear and/or time-invariant systems
- Convolution and frequency response
- Causal systems
- BIBO stable systems
- Combinations of LTI systems
- Inverse Fourier transform (to recover $h[n]$ from $H(e^{j\omega})$)

---

### 2. Getting the Impulse Response from the Frequency Response (Inverse DTFT)
- Inverse DTFT formula:
  $$ h[n] = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} H(e^{j\omega})\,e^{j\omega n}\,d\omega $$
- Using the DTFT definition:
  $$ H(e^{j\omega}) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} h[k]e^{-j\omega k} $$
- Substitute into the inverse transform and swap sum/integral:
  $$
  h[n]
  = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} h[k]\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} e^{j\omega(n-k)}\,d\omega\right)
  $$
- Key orthogonality result:
  $$
  \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} e^{j\omega(n-k)}\,d\omega =
  \begin{cases}
  1, & k=n \\
  0, & k\ne n
  \end{cases}
  = \delta[n-k]
  $$
- Therefore:
  $$ h[n] = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} h[k]\delta[n-k] = h[n] $$

---

### 3. Ideal Lowpass Filter in Frequency Domain
- Ideal lowpass magnitude response (rectangular in $\omega$):
  $$
  H_{LP,\text{ideal}}(e^{j\omega}) =
  \begin{cases}
  1, & |\omega|\le \omega_c \\
  0, & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
  $$

---

### 4. Ideal Lowpass Impulse Response (Sinc Form)
- Apply inverse DTFT:
  $$ h_{LP,\text{ideal}}[n] = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\omega_c}^{\omega_c} e^{j\omega n}\,d\omega $$
- For $n\ne 0$:
  $$
  h_{LP,\text{ideal}}[n]
  = \frac{e^{j\omega_c n}-e^{-j\omega_c n}}{j2\pi n}
  = \frac{\sin(\omega_c n)}{\pi n}
  $$
- For $n=0$:
  $$ h_{LP,\text{ideal}}[0] = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\omega_c}^{\omega_c} 1\,d\omega = \frac{\omega_c}{\pi} $$
- Recognize the (discrete-time) sinc shape:
  $$ \text{sinc}(x) = \frac{\sin(\pi x)}{\pi x} $$

---

### 5. Noncausality / Stability Note for Ideal Lowpass
- The ideal lowpass has infinite-length $h[n]$ (sinc tails)
- Notes indicate a divergence comparison showing it is not absolutely summable:
  $$ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\sin(\omega_c n)}{\pi n}\right| \;\text{diverges} $$
- Motivation: we need an *approximate* lowpass filter

---

### 6. Approximate Lowpass by Truncation (Windowing)
- Truncated (finite-length) approximation:
  $$
  \tilde{h}_{LP}[n] =
  \begin{cases}
  \frac{\omega_c}{\pi}, & n=0 \\
  \frac{\sin(\omega_c n)}{\pi n}, & |n|\le L,\; n\ne 0 \\
  0, & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
  $$
- Make it causal by delaying (shift right by $L$):
  $$ h_{LP}[n] = \tilde{h}_{LP}[n-L] $$
- Time shift property in frequency:
  $$ H(e^{j\omega}) = \tilde{H}(e^{j\omega})e^{-j\omega L} $$
- Result: **linear phase** term $-\,\omega L$

---

### 7. Ideal Bandpass from Two Lowpass Filters
- Construct bandpass by subtraction:
  $$ h_{BP,\text{ideal}}[n] = h_{LP,\omega_u}[n] - h_{LP,\omega_l}[n] $$
- Corresponding frequency response is a “rectangular ring”:
  passband for $\omega_l \le |\omega| \le \omega_u$

---

### 8. Modulation Property (Frequency Shifting)
- Multiply in time by a cosine to shift spectra:
  $$ 2h[n]\cos(\omega_0 n) = h[n]e^{j\omega_0 n} + h[n]e^{-j\omega_0 n} $$
- DTFT shift result (shown in the notes):
  $$ \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} h[n]e^{-j(\omega-\omega_0)n} = H(e^{j(\omega-\omega_0)}) $$
- Used to move a lowpass prototype to a bandpass location

---

### 9. Highpass from Lowpass via Modulation
- Key identity in notes:
  $$ h_{HP}[n] = h_{LP}[n]e^{j\pi n} $$
- Interprets as shifting the spectrum by $\pi$ (moving passband toward Nyquist)

---

### 10. Bandpass Parameter Relationships
- Center frequency:
  $$ \omega_0 = \frac{\omega_l+\omega_u}{2} $$
- Bandwidth:
  $$ 2\omega_c = \omega_u-\omega_l
  \quad\Rightarrow\quad
  \omega_c = \frac{\omega_u-\omega_l}{2} $$
