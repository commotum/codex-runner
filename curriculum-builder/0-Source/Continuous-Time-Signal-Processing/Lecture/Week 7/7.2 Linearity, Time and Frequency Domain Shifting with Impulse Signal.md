## Lecture Outline (Fourier Transform Properties: Linearity, Shifted Impulses, and the Transform of a Cosine)

### 1. Why Fourier Transform Properties Matter
- Fourier transform lets us map a signal from the time domain $x(t)$ to the frequency domain $X(j\omega)$.
- In many problems, the signal is scaled, shifted, or written in a more complicated form.
- Knowing the transform properties makes those problems much easier to solve.

---

### 2. Fourier Transform Definition
- Forward Fourier transform:
  $$ X(j\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- Inverse Fourier transform:
  $$ x(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} X(j\omega)e^{j\omega t}\,d\omega $$

---

### 3. Linearity Property
- If:
  $$ x_1(t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; X_1(j\omega) $$
  $$ x_2(t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; X_2(j\omega) $$
- Then:
  $$ A_1x_1(t) + A_2x_2(t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; A_1X_1(j\omega) + A_2X_2(j\omega) $$
- This is the **linearity property** of the Fourier transform.

---

### 4. Proof Sketch for Linearity
- Start from the definition:
  $$ \mathcal{F}\{A_1x_1(t)+A_2x_2(t)\}
     = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \left(A_1x_1(t)+A_2x_2(t)\right)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- Split the integral:
  $$ = A_1\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x_1(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt
     + A_2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x_2(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- Therefore:
  $$ \mathcal{F}\{A_1x_1(t)+A_2x_2(t)\} = A_1X_1(j\omega)+A_2X_2(j\omega) $$

---

### 5. Fourier Transform of a Shifted Impulse in Time
- Let:
  $$ x(t) = A\delta(t-t_0) $$
- Apply the transform:
  $$ X(j\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} A\delta(t-t_0)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt $$
- Using the sifting property of the delta function:
  $$ X(j\omega) = Ae^{-j\omega t_0} $$
- So the transform pair is:
  $$ A\delta(t-t_0) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; Ae^{-j\omega t_0} $$

---

### 6. Meaning of the Shifted-Impulse Result
- A time-domain impulse shifted to $t=t_0$ becomes a complex exponential in frequency.
- The shift $t_0$ appears in the phase term:
  $$ e^{-j\omega t_0} $$

---

### 7. Shifted Impulse in the Frequency Domain
- Suppose the signal is given in the frequency domain as:
  $$ X(j\omega) = 2\pi A\,\delta(\omega-\omega_0) $$
- Apply the inverse Fourier transform:
  $$ x(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} 2\pi A\,\delta(\omega-\omega_0)e^{j\omega t}\,d\omega $$
- Using the sifting property:
  $$ x(t) = Ae^{j\omega_0 t} $$
- So the transform pair is:
  $$ Ae^{j\omega_0 t} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; 2\pi A\,\delta(\omega-\omega_0) $$

---

### 8. Dual Interpretation
- A single frequency-domain impulse at $\omega=\omega_0$ corresponds to a pure complex exponential in time.
- This is the frequency-domain counterpart of the shifted time-domain impulse result.

---

### 9. Example: Fourier Transform of a Cosine
- Let:
  $$ x(t) = A\cos(\omega_0 t) $$
- Use Euler’s identity:
  $$ \cos(\omega_0 t) = \frac{1}{2}e^{j\omega_0 t} + \frac{1}{2}e^{-j\omega_0 t} $$
- Therefore:
  $$ x(t) = \frac{A}{2}e^{j\omega_0 t} + \frac{A}{2}e^{-j\omega_0 t} $$

---

### 10. Apply Linearity and the Exponential Transform Pair
- From the previous property:
  $$ \frac{A}{2}e^{j\omega_0 t} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; A\pi\,\delta(\omega-\omega_0) $$
- Also:
  $$ \frac{A}{2}e^{-j\omega_0 t} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; A\pi\,\delta(\omega+\omega_0) $$
- So:
  $$ A\cos(\omega_0 t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; A\pi\left[\delta(\omega-\omega_0)+\delta(\omega+\omega_0)\right] $$

---

### 11. Interpretation of the Cosine Spectrum
- A real cosine in time has only one frequency $\omega_0$.
- In the frequency domain, that appears as two impulses:
  $$ \omega = +\omega_0 \quad \text{and} \quad \omega = -\omega_0 $$
- These two symmetric impulses represent the positive- and negative-frequency components of the cosine.

---

### 12. Main Takeaways
- **Linearity**:
  $$ A_1x_1(t)+A_2x_2(t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; A_1X_1(j\omega)+A_2X_2(j\omega) $$
- **Shifted time-domain impulse**:
  $$ A\delta(t-t_0) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; Ae^{-j\omega t_0} $$
- **Shifted frequency-domain impulse**:
  $$ 2\pi A\,\delta(\omega-\omega_0) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; Ae^{j\omega_0 t} $$
- **Cosine transform**:
  $$ A\cos(\omega_0 t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; A\pi\left[\delta(\omega-\omega_0)+\delta(\omega+\omega_0)\right] $$

---

### 13. Big Picture
- These properties let you avoid doing a full integral every time.
- Once a few basic transform pairs are known, many Fourier transform problems can be solved by:
  - rewriting the signal
  - applying linearity
  - using delta-function and exponential transform pairs
